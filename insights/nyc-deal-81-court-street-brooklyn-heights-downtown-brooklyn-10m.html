River Drive LLC Takes Down Brooklyn Heights / Downtown Brooklyn's 81 Court Street for $10.5M at $700 a Foot

The $10.5 million closing in Brooklyn Heights / Downtown Brooklyn sets a fresh $700 a square foot benchmark across 15,000 square feet.

Across from Borough Hall and the Kings County Supreme Court on Court Street, six-story elevator buildings with ground-floor retail cater to a captive ecosystem of trial attorneys, title companies, and neighborhood retailers. On August 20, 2026, River Drive LLC paid $10.5 million all-cash ($700 per square foot) to acquire the 15,000-square-foot commercial building at 81 Court Street from Investors Court Street LLC—just 8.7 percent below the $11.5 million price recorded in November 2020. When the deed closed on August 20, 2026, River Drive LLC had officially taken down 81 Court Street from Investors Court Street LLC for $10.5 million, with Brooklyn Commercial Advisory brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 15,000-square-foot building last traded in November 2020 for $11.5 million. Stepping in at $700 a square foot shaves 8.7 percent off that prior valuation, giving River Drive LLC a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle.

Why Boutique Brooklyn Courthouse Buildings Hold Their Value

Just as telling as the purchase price is what did not hit the city register alongside the deed: a senior mortgage. River Drive LLC closed 81 Court Street with $10.5 million of unencumbered equity—or an unsecured corporate line—allowing the buyers to move with immediate execution certainty.

When financing contingencies can stall a New York closing for sixty days, showing up with $10.5 million in cash at $700 a square foot is the sharpest weapon a buyer can bring to the negotiating table—leaving the sponsorship free to recapitalize in the senior debt market once initial leasing and business-plan milestones are locked in.

From Our Desk

When we look at 81 Court Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and capital structure. River Drive LLC didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Brooklyn Heights / Downtown Brooklyn footage at $700 a square foot and structured the stack to match the asset's timeline. While commodity glass office towers took 40 percent haircuts, a 15,000-square-foot brick building on Court Street between Livingston and Schermerhorn streets cleared $700 a foot all-cash—within 9 percent of its 2020 peak.
