Northmarq Lends $19.1M Through Freddie Mac’s Optigo Platform for a 154-Unit Community in Dover Pennsylvania

A $19.1 million refinancing for Dover Run Apartments in Dover, Pennsylvania, shows how Freddie Mac’s Optigo platform is being used to secure long-term multifami

Ben Rohr September 28, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Northmarq’s Philadelphia Debt + Equity team has arranged a $19.1 million refinancing for Dover Run Apartments, a 154-unit garden-style community at 2670 Springhouse Bend in Dover, Pennsylvania. The deal was completed on behalf of the borrower, Boyd Wilson, and secured through Freddie Mac’s Optigo platform. The transaction matters because it demonstrates that agency multifamily lending remains available for well-located, recently built rental properties even as broader lending conditions are described as challenging. The financing was structured on a five-year term with a 35-year amortization schedule, a profile that points to long-term debt stability rather than short-term bridge capital.

The material facts are narrow but specific. The loan amount is $19.1 million, and the property was built in 2019. Dover Run consists of 13 buildings with a mix of one- and two-bedroom units. Each unit includes modern finishes, an in-unit washer and dryer, and a private balcony or patio. Community amenities include a clubhouse, fitness center, dog park, and walking trail. The property has access to Route 30 and Interstate 83, connecting residents to employment centers in York, Harrisburg, and Lancaster. The five-year term and 35-year amortization schedule are the only financing mechanics disclosed in the source, and no interest rate, loan-to-value ratio, or debt yield was provided.

The evidence comes from a single secondary source, Yield PRO, published on September 28, 2026. The article includes a direct quote from John Banas, who said, “In a challenging lending environment, our team and the team at Boyd Wilson were on top of our game to secure great long-term financing.” The source also identifies the Northmarq team members as John Banas, Kris Wood, David Singer, and Jeff Steigerwalt. Northmarq is described as one of the largest privately held commercial real estate firms in the United States, with more than 50 offices, a loan servicing portfolio of over $80 billion, and $91.3 billion in transactions completed over the past four years. Those figures are presented as company-provided context rather than independently verified market data.

The sector implication is that Freddie Mac’s Optigo platform continues to function as a liquidity channel for multifamily refinancings in secondary Pennsylvania markets. Dover is not a primary gateway market, yet the property’s 2019 construction, unit mix, and highway access appear to have supported agency execution. The deal also highlights the role of mortgage banking intermediaries such as Northmarq in connecting borrowers to agency capital. However, the source does not provide comparable transactions, cap rates, rent rolls, or occupancy data, so the pricing and underwriting strength of this specific loan cannot be assessed from the available evidence.

Several limitations should be noted. The dossier contains only one source, and no primary loan documents or Freddie Mac disclosures were reviewed. The borrower’s financial position, the property’s operating performance, and the use of proceeds beyond refinancing are not disclosed. The article does not state whether the prior debt was maturing, whether cash-out was involved, or how the new loan compares to the original financing. What to watch is whether similar five-year, 35-year amortization structures become more common for recently built suburban multifamily assets, and whether Freddie Mac’s Optigo volume holds up if broader lending conditions remain constrained.
