George Comfort & Sons Refinances 200 Madison for $386M

A $386-million floating-rate refinancing of 200 Madison Ave. signals lender appetite for well-located, amenity-rich office assets in the Grand Central district.

Ben Rohr September 24, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

George Comfort & Sons, alongside partners Loeb Partners Realty and Jamestown, has completed a $386-million refinancing of 200 Madison Ave., a 750,000-square-foot office tower in the Grand Central district. The transaction matters because it provides a concrete, single-asset data point on how lenders are underwriting Manhattan office properties at a time when the broader sector remains under scrutiny. New York Life provided the five-year floating-rate loan, structured with a three-year initial term and two 12-month extension options. The proceeds will be used to pay off the property's existing debt and fund ongoing leasing, according to the source.

The mechanics of the deal are straightforward but notable. The loan is floating-rate, which exposes the borrower to interest-rate movement over the term, while the extension options give the sponsorship flexibility if capital markets or leasing conditions shift. The refinancing follows an August agreement with Havas Health for a 15-year extension and headquarters expansion, which grew the global firm's footprint at the property to 254,118 square feet. That leasing event appears to be a key underwriting anchor for the refinancing, though the source does not disclose loan-to-value, interest rate, or other financial terms.

The evidence comes from a single secondary source, Connect CRE, which reported the transaction in full. The article quotes George Comfort & Sons president and CEO Peter S. Duncan as saying the refinancing "underscores the lending community's confidence in office assets like 200 Madison Ave. that are defined by top-tier amenities, unrivaled location and strong sponsorship." The source also identifies the brokerage teams involved: Jonathan Estreich, Peter A. Duncan and Egor Petrov of Estreich & Company, and Adam Spies, Adam Doneger and Willis Robbins of Newmark assisted in arranging the transaction. No additional documents, lender statements, or public filings are cited in the dossier.

For the commercial real estate sector, the deal suggests that institutional lenders remain willing to commit significant capital to office buildings with demonstrated tenant demand and established sponsorship. The Grand Central district location and the recent Havas Health expansion are the two most concrete signals of asset quality in the source. However, the floating-rate structure also implies a degree of caution: lenders and borrowers may prefer shorter, more flexible terms rather than long-dated fixed-rate debt in the current environment. The refinancing does not, by itself, establish a broader market trend, but it is a useful reference point for similar Class A office assets in transit-oriented Manhattan submarkets.

The main limitation is the thin evidentiary base. The dossier contains one full-text source, and several material facts are uncorroborated. The source does not disclose the prior debt amount, the property's valuation, occupancy beyond the Havas Health footprint, or the loan spread. It also does not state whether the refinancing includes reserves for tenant improvements or leasing commissions. Those unknowns mean the transaction should be read as an anecdote rather than a definitive market signal. What to watch next is whether other Grand Central district office owners complete similar refinancings, and whether the Havas Health expansion translates into sustained occupancy and rent collection at 200 Madison Ave.
