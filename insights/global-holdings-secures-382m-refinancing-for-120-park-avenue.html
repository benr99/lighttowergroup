Global Holdings secures $382m refinancing for 120 Park Avenue

Wells Fargo and LBBW co-originated a $382.4 million loan for the 620,000-square-foot office tower near Grand Central Terminal, replacing $335 million in prior f

Ben Rohr September 08, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Global Holdings, the international real estate development and investment firm led by chairman and founder Eyal Ofer, has refinanced 120 Park Avenue, its 620,000-square-foot trophy office tower located steps from Grand Central Terminal on Park Avenue in midtown Manhattan. Wells Fargo and LBBW co-originated the $382.4 million loan, which includes $360.5 million in initial proceeds and replaces the property's existing $335 million financing.

The refinancing follows reported leasing momentum at the property. Bloomberg occupies approximately 80 percent of the building's rentable area, nearly 500,000 square feet across 20 floors, and signed a long-term renewal at the end of 2025 extending its tenancy through February 2040. Global Holdings is also advancing capital improvements at 120 Park Avenue, including a lobby renovation, privately owned public space improvements, elevator modernization, and recently completed window upgrades on the fifth and sixth floors.

The transaction suggests lenders remain willing to underwrite well-located Manhattan office assets with a dominant, long-term anchor tenant. The new loan's initial proceeds of $360.5 million exceed the $335 million prior financing, indicating additional liquidity was available against the property. However, the dossier does not provide loan pricing, term length, loan-to-value ratio, or other underwriting metrics, so the refinancing's broader market signal cannot be quantified from this evidence alone.

What remains unknown is whether the additional proceeds will fund the announced capital improvements, return equity to ownership, or serve other purposes. The single-source summary also does not disclose the prior lender, the exact closing date, or any subordinate financing. Because the evidence level is a single summary, the refinancing should be read as a discrete event rather than a basis for broader claims about the Manhattan office financing market.
