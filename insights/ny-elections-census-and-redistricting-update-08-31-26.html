NY Elections, Census and Redistricting Update 08/31/26

Brooklyn Democrats changed party rules before a leadership vote, Democrats asked the State Elections Board to investigate a Trump event, and an even-year voting

Ben Rohr September 01, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

This week's New York elections update centers on three developments: a contested rule change by Brooklyn Democrats ahead of a county leadership election, a Democratic complaint asking the State Board of Elections to investigate a Trump event in Nassau County, and a legal challenge to the state's even-year voting law as a voting rights violation. The update also notes State Board of Elections 2026 activity, a free CLE on voting rights, Attorney General preclearance activity, and census-related developments.

In Brooklyn, reformers led by New Kings Democrats and the "Brooklyn Can't Wait" coalition secured 22 of 42 District Leader seats in the June 2026 primaries and backed Julio Peña for county committee chair. On August 25, current chair Rodneyse Bichotte Hermelyn pushed through rule changes adding dozens of voting members to the executive committee. Opponents include Governor Kathy Hochul, Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Attorney General Letitia James, and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries. A Kings County Supreme Court judge denied a motion to halt the vote, but the coalition refiled after passage, arguing the changes violate party bylaws and New York Election Law § 2-106. An order to show cause is set for September 2.

Separately, New York State Democratic Chair Jay Jacobs filed an official complaint on August 28, 2026 with the Division of Election Law Enforcement and the Public Campaign Finance Board. The complaint alleges Nassau County and federal resources were improperly used to promote gubernatorial candidate Bruce Blakeman during a mid-August Trump event at the Nassau County David Mack Center for Training and Intelligence. Blakeman described Trump's visit as a rally and endorsement. The dossier notes the event cost Nassau County approximately $1 million, far above the $9,000 contribution limit for statewide offices under N.Y. Elec. Law § 1.

What remains unknown is how the September 2 Brooklyn court hearing will resolve the rule-change dispute, whether the State Board of Elections will open a formal investigation into the Trump event, and what specific relief the even-year voting law challengers are seeking. The dossier provides only one full-text source, so corroboration of the $1 million cost figure and the alleged coordination between federal and county resources is not yet available.
