NYC Council Passes 9/11 Remembrance and Education Legislation Ahead of 25th Anniversary

The New York City Council approved measures to co-name 26 thoroughfares and public places honoring 9/11 victims and to call for a statewide 9/11 curriculum in s

Ben Rohr September 11, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Ahead of the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks, the New York City Council approved new legislation commemorating the tragic day and passing its lessons to new generations. The package includes Introduction 1020, sponsored by Speaker Julie Menin, which would co-name 26 thoroughfares and public places with titles honoring the memory of those lost and families impacted by the attacks. The full list of co-namings is documented on a new Council webpage dedicated to 9/11 remembrance, featuring interactive maps and data providing context for each honorary street name.

The Council also approved Resolution 7-A, sponsored by Council Member Joann Ariola, Speaker Julie Menin, and Council Member Rita Joseph, calling on the New York State Legislature to pass and the Governor to sign legislation incorporating a 9/11 curriculum in all New York schools. Council Member Ariola emphasized the need to teach the day's pain, loss, heroism, and unity to future generations. Separately, Speaker Menin and the Council have spearheaded a citywide initiative to honor FDNY and NYPD personnel who died on September 11 with red or blue street signs replacing existing green signs.

The legislative package extends beyond 9/11 remembrance. The Council voted on resolutions to celebrate the Feast of San Gennaro and the Ferragosto Festival annually in September, declare March 6 as Carmen de Lavallade Day, recognize the life and legacy of Alvin Ailey, and establish a registry documenting all known burial sites historically associated with formerly enslaved persons or historically African American communities citywide. The Council also passed a measure to co-name seven additional public places with commemorative titles and voted to confirm three mayoral appointments.

What remains unknown from the primary source is the final vote tally for each measure, the timeline for state legislative action on the 9/11 curriculum resolution, and the specific criteria used to select the 26 co-named thoroughfares. The source does not indicate whether the co-naming legislation requires mayoral approval or when the new street signs would be installed.
