Crusoe signs $13bn deal with Jane Street - report

Quantitative trading firm Jane Street has reportedly signed a $13 billion cloud computing contract with Crusoe, marking the largest cloud agreement for the data

Ben Rohr September 04, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Quantitative trading firm Jane Street has reportedly signed a $13 billion cloud computing contract with Crusoe, a data center developer and cloud provider. Under the agreement, Crusoe will give Jane Street access to GPUs via its cloud platform, with the contract set to run for five years. The reported deal matters because it would represent the largest cloud contract signed by Crusoe to date, and it signals deepening demand for specialized AI compute capacity from sophisticated financial firms that are increasingly competing for scarce GPU resources.

The material facts remain limited but specific. The $13 billion figure was reported by Bloomberg, citing people with knowledge of the deal, and relayed by Data Center Dynamics. Crusoe declined to comment to Bloomberg. The report does not state which data center will host the compute capacity. Crusoe operates or is developing facilities across the US in Texas and Wyoming, and has been linked to projects in Missouri and Virginia. The company also leases capacity in third-party data centers for its cloud platform. According to Crusoe's website, it has 13.6 million square feet of data centers and 4.9GW of capacity, though how much of that is operational is unclear.

The evidence base is narrow: a single secondary source read in full, which itself relies on Bloomberg reporting and unnamed people familiar with the deal. No primary documentation, regulatory filing, or on-the-record confirmation from either Crusoe or Jane Street is available in the dossier. The source is Data Center Dynamics, a trade publication with tier-2 authority, and the article was published on September 4, 2026. The absence of corroboration from a second independent outlet or direct company statement means the $13 billion figure and five-year term should be treated as reported rather than confirmed.

The sector implications are notable. Crusoe has previously signed major capacity deals with Oracle, Microsoft, and Meta, but those agreements were for physical data center space rather than use of Crusoe's cloud platform. This reported Jane Street contract would be different in kind, representing cloud platform access rather than colocation or build-to-suit space. Jane Street has been significantly expanding its compute capacity of late: it signed a $6 billion AI cloud agreement with CoreWeave in April of this year, covering capacity across multiple facilities and including Nvidia Vera Rubin hardware. In June, reports emerged that Jane Street was even looking into establishing its own data center with between 100MW and 200MW of capacity. Other quantitative trading firms, including IMC and Hudson River Trading, have also signed notable cloud contracts in recent months.

Several limitations and unknowns remain. The hosting location for the compute capacity has not been stated, and it is unclear how much of Crusoe's 4.9GW portfolio is operational versus under development. Bloomberg previously reported that Crusoe was seeking a $3 billion funding round at a $30 billion valuation, and the publication states that interest in that round has increased since the Jane Street deal was reported. However, no financing terms, valuation changes, or deal structure details are confirmed in the available evidence. What to watch next includes any official confirmation from Crusoe or Jane Street, disclosure of the hosting site, and whether the reported contract influences Crusoe's fundraising trajectory or Jane Street's broader compute strategy.
