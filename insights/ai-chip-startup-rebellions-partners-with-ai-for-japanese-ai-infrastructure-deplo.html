AI chip startup Rebellions partners with ai& for Japanese AI infrastructure deployment

South Korean AI chip startup Rebellions has partnered with Japanese AI company ai& to deploy its RebelRack AI infrastructure platform, with ai& planning to purc

Ben Rohr September 17, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

South Korean AI chip startup Rebellions has partnered with Japanese AI company ai& for deployments of its RebelRack AI infrastructure platform. The infrastructure will initially be deployed in ai&’s Tokyo data center, with ai& planning to purchase up to 100 units. Rebellions said the deployment would scale alongside ai&’s broader data center buildout, for which ai& has already committed $2 billion in infrastructure capital.

Five sites are expected to be operational by the end of 2026, with ai& targeting 40MW of capacity by year-end 2027. Once deployed, the RebelRack infrastructure will be made available to enterprises, government institutions, and developers across Japan, expanding locally available compute infrastructure to support the Japanese government’s sovereign AI ambitions. Rebellions, founded in 2020, specializes in AI inference accelerators offering energy efficiency and low-latency performance. The company began mass-producing its Atom and Atom Max NPU in 2023 and released its RebelRack and RebelRock AI platforms in March 2026. That same month, Rebellions raised $400 million in a pre-IPO funding round led by Mirae Asset Financial Group and the Korea National Growth Fund, bringing total raised to $850 million and valuing the company at approximately $2.34 billion.

The partnership positions Rebellions to serve Japanese enterprise, government, and developer demand for efficient inference capacity. Rebellions CEO Sunghyun Park said lowering the unit cost of serving tokens gives providers room to create new pricing tiers and support more applications. ai& CEO David Bennett said Rebellions fits ai&’s heterogeneous infrastructure model and builds on the same open-source frameworks its engineers already use. The arrangement could expand inference options for customers in Japan while supporting sovereign AI goals.

What remains unknown is the commercial value or timing of the up to 100 RebelRack units, the specific performance or pricing of the hardware, and whether the deployment will extend beyond ai&’s Tokyo data center. The dossier also does not specify contractual terms, revenue impact for Rebellions, or how the partnership will be affected by Rebellions’ pre-IPO status and future fundraising plans.
