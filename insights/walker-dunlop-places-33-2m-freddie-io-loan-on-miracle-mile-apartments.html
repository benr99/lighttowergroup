Walker & Dunlop Places $33.2M Freddie IO Loan on Miracle Mile Apartments

The fixed-rate, interest-only structure is the reveal; the missing LTV and rate are the next test.

Walker & Dunlop arranged a $33.2 million Freddie Mac acquisition loan for Palm Court Apartments with a fixed rate for the full term and interest-only payments throughout. The financing structure is the story before the property address.

The Walker & Dunlop team led by Bryan Frazier, Blake Hockenbury, Steven Paskover, Sheri Blackburn, and Dillon Hoffman arranged the Freddie Mac loan on behalf of Prime Finance. Palm Court Apartments is a 132-unit, four-story, Mediterranean-inspired rental community at 740 S. Burnside Avenue in Los Angeles’s Miracle Mile.

Full-term interest-only means no principal amortization for the life of the loan. Prime Finance keeps more cash flow from the property, but the $33.2 million balance remains outstanding at maturity. Freddie Mac is therefore carrying the duration risk on a fixed-rate loan tied to an infill rental property. The reported facts do not include the interest rate, spread, loan term, LTV, DSCR, purchase price, or cap rate.

At 132 units, the financing equals about $251,500 per apartment. That is arithmetic drawn from the reported loan amount and unit count. It does not establish leverage without a purchase price, and it cannot determine debt service without a rate.

Why this structure now? The asset sits near Cedars-Sinai and Kaiser Permanente. It also has access to UCLA, USC, and the Metro D Line extension, which supports long-term renter demand. Frazier’s quote points to an “irreplaceable infill location” and “durable renter demand.” That combination gives Freddie Mac a credit story. The full-term IO feature is what a credit committee would have to accept on its own terms, because it leaves principal outstanding for the entire loan.

The next test is the gap between the loan amount and the purchase price. If Prime Finance paid a cap rate that required full-term IO to make the deal work, the loan is a statement about pricing. If Freddie Mac’s rate and LTV are conservative, the structure is simply agency liquidity doing its normal work for top-tier Los Angeles multifamily collateral.
