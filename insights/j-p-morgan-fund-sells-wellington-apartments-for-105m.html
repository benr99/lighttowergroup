J.P. Morgan Fund Sells Wellington Apartments For $105M

A J.P. Morgan Asset Management fund sold the 366-unit Polo Lakes complex in Wellington to TA Realty for $105.4M after 24 years of ownership, more than doubling

Ben Rohr September 23, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

A fund controlled by J.P. Morgan Asset Management has sold the 366-unit Polo Lakes apartment complex in Wellington, Florida, to an affiliate of TA Realty for $105.4M, according to property records provided by Vizzda. The transaction matters because it shows a major institutional owner monetizing a long-held suburban multifamily asset at a price that more than doubles its original investment, even as the South Florida rental market works through elevated construction and flat rents. The sale also signals that well-located, lower-density properties can still attract intense buyer competition despite broader market softness.

The property at 1950 Polo Lake Blvd. was acquired by J.P. Morgan in 2002 for $47.4M from The Bainbridge Cos., which developed the 27-building complex in 2000. The 531,000-square-foot asset sits on 27 acres with four three-story buildings and 23 two-story buildings, averaging about 14 units per acre. Still Hunter, a managing director at Walker & Dunlop who helped broker the deal, said the sale process was "extremely competitive," with Boston-based TA Realty beating out 21 bidders. Hunter attributed buyer interest to the asset's "excellent Wellington location" and attributes that "would be difficult to replicate today," including larger units averaging 1,300 square feet and low-density design. Monthly rents range from $1,974 to $5,341, according to Apartments.com, with some units featuring two-level townhomes and attached garages.

The evidence for this transaction comes from a single full-text report by Bisnow, which cites property records from Vizzda and market data from Colliers. The source is a secondary trade publication, and neither J.P. Morgan nor TA Realty provided on-the-record comment; J.P. Morgan declined to comment, while TA Realty did not immediately respond. TA Realty is described as an active private investor with $19B of assets under management, according to its website. The Walker & Dunlop team included Hunter, Kaya Robert Suarez, Luke Duffack, Leigh Ann Gerke, and Bennett Hopkins, according to Hunter's LinkedIn post. Because the dossier contains no independent corroboration from public filings or buyer statements, the reported price and competitive bidding details should be treated as sourced but not independently verified.

The sale lands at a transitional moment for South Florida multifamily. Rents averaged $2,590 a month in the second quarter, essentially unchanged from the prior year, while construction remained elevated at 29,826 units under construction, up from 28,457 a year earlier. Yet investment activity rose 53% quarter-over-quarter to $1.3B, and Palm Beach County quarterly sales volume reached $320M, tripling from the second quarter of 2025, according to Colliers. TA Realty paid $288,000 per unit for Polo Lakes, below the Palm Beach County average of $358,000 per unit, which Colliers described as a record high. That gap suggests the buyer secured pricing below the countywide average even amid rising transaction volumes, possibly reflecting the property's age, unit mix, or the competitive dynamics of a 22-bidder process.

Several limitations frame this analysis. The dossier does not include information on the property's occupancy, net operating income, capital expenditures, or financing terms, so the implied yield or return profile cannot be assessed. The reported $105.4M sale price and $47.4M acquisition price imply a gross gain of roughly $58M over 24 years, but that figure excludes any capital invested during the holding period and is not stated in the source. The absence of buyer or seller commentary also leaves the strategic rationale unconfirmed beyond broker statements. What to watch next is whether TA Realty pursues renovations or repositioning at Polo Lakes, and whether the Palm Beach County per-unit pricing trend continues to diverge from the broader South Florida market as construction deliveries peak.
