JSB Capital Group Secures $238M Refinancing for Class A Multifamily Community in Miami Submarket

JSB Capital Group has received a $238 million refinancing loan from Torchlight Investors for The Landmark South, a 631-unit Class A multifamily community in Doral, Fla., a submarket of Miami. Walker & Dunlop arranged the transaction on behalf of the borrower. The deal matters because it shows continued lender appetite for well-leased, Class A multifamily assets in South Florida even as the broader commercial real estate market navigates higher interest rates and refinancing pressure. The new financing is a floating-rate, interest-only bridge loan, a structure that suggests the borrower is seeking flexibility while positioning the asset for a longer-term capital solution.

The mechanics of the refinancing sit within a layered capital stack. JSB acquired the community in 2021 for $255 million from Canyon Partners. In 2023, JSB obtained a $154.1 million Freddie Mac loan as part of a recapitalization arranged by JLL. That loan, which was scheduled to mature in June 2028, was paired with a $65.8 million preferred equity investment from Pensam. The new $238 million Torchlight loan therefore refinances an asset that already carried agency debt and preferred equity, indicating a recapitalization rather than a simple first-mortgage replacement. The floating-rate, interest-only structure also implies that the borrower is accepting near-term rate exposure in exchange for lower initial debt service and greater prepayment flexibility.

The property itself supports the financing. The Landmark South includes one-, two- and three-bedroom layouts with in-unit laundry, walk-in closets and granite countertops. The developer delivered the property in two phases, with 418 units completed in 2017 and the remaining 213 units completed in 2021. AECOM-Canyon Partners developed the second phase, while Moss & Associates served as general contractor. Shared amenities include a swimming pool, a fitness center, a clubhouse and a playground. Bryten Real Estate Partners manages the property. Occupancy at The Landmark South was 93 percent in August. Located at 6055 NW 105th Court in Doral, the property is 11 miles from Miami International Airport, and major retailers such as ALDI, Ace Hardware and Publix are within walking distance. Those locational and operational fundamentals are the kind of evidence lenders typically require for a bridge refinancing of this size.

The transaction has broader implications for the Miami-area multifamily market. Doral is a well-established submarket with proximity to Miami International Airport and a strong retail base, which supports renter demand. A $238 million refinancing for a single asset signals that institutional lenders such as Torchlight Investors remain willing to deploy capital into stabilized Class A multifamily in South Florida, even when the debt is floating-rate. The involvement of Walker & Dunlop, with a nine-person team led by Aaron Appel, Michael Stepniewski, Jonathan Schwartz, Keith Kurland, Adam Schwartz, Dustin Stolly, Sean Reimer, Sean Bastian and Stanley Cayre, also reflects the depth of brokerage capacity focused on large multifamily debt placements. For owners who acquired assets near the 2021 pricing peak, refinancings like this can provide a bridge to more favorable long-term rates or a future sale.

The evidence base for this analysis is limited to a single trade-publication report from Yield PRO, so several important details remain unknown. The dossier does not state the interest rate, spread, term length, loan-to-value ratio, or debt yield on the new Torchlight loan. It also does not disclose whether the Freddie Mac loan and Pensam preferred equity remain in place or were repaid as part of the refinancing. The 2021 acquisition price of $255 million and the 2023 recapitalization figures provide context, but no current valuation is given. Occupancy is reported only as a single August figure, with no rent growth, concessions, or historical occupancy trend. Investors should watch for subsequent disclosures on the loan's pricing and term, any changes to the capital stack, and whether the floating-rate exposure is hedged. Without those details, the refinancing should be read as a positive but incomplete signal of lender confidence in the asset and the Doral submarket.
