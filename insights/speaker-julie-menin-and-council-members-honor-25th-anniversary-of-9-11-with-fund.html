Speaker Julie Menin and Council Members Honor 25th Anniversary of 9/11 with Funding Announcement and Wreath-Laying Ceremony

Ahead of the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks, New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin and Council Members gathered at the 9/11 Memorial to honor

Ben Rohr September 04, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Ahead of the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks, New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin and Council Members gathered at the 9/11 Memorial on the morning of September 3, 2026, to honor the nearly 3,000 lives lost and announce $1 million in funding for the 9/11 Memorial & Museum. The funding, secured in the Fiscal Year 2027 Budget, will support free field trips for New York City students, with a focus on eighth-grade classes, and expand related educational programming.

The Council-secured funding will go toward guided, self-guided, and virtual field trips for NYC public school students, free audio guides for NYC student groups, access to the 9/11 Anniversary Digital Learning Experience, and related training for NYC educators. Beth Hillman, President & CEO of the 9/11 Memorial & Museum, said there are 100 million Americans alive today who are too young to remember 9/11, underscoring why the educational programming is vital. Speaker Menin said her own career in public service was born from the resilience, bravery, and unity she witnessed from New Yorkers in the aftermath of 9/11.

The announcement reflects a policy emphasis on intergenerational remembrance: nearly one-third of Americans were born after September 11, 2001, or are too young to remember the day firsthand. By targeting eighth graders, the initiative aims to ensure that "Never Forget" remains a promise passed from one generation to the next. The same City Council announcement also noted that Speaker Menin and the Council secured $4 million in additional funding for the Department of Investigation, though the release did not connect that funding to the 9/11 Memorial initiative.

What remains unknown from the single primary source is the total number of students expected to participate, the timeline for distributing the $1 million, and how the virtual and in-person field trip components will be allocated. The release also does not specify whether the $4 million for the Department of Investigation is related to 9/11 health or air-quality inquiries, despite Council Member Shekar Krishnan referencing calls for answers on what city government knew about air quality during and after the attack.
