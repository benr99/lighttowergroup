New York Life Provides $386M Refi for 200 Madison Avenue

A joint venture between George Comfort & Sons, Loeb Partners Realty and Jamestown has secured a $386 million floating-rate loan from New York Life to refinance

Ben Rohr September 24, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

A joint venture between George Comfort & Sons, Loeb Partners Realty and Jamestown has secured a $386 million loan to refinance its Midtown East office tower at 200 Madison Avenue, Commercial Observer reported on September 23, 2026. The deal matters because it signals that at least one major institutional lender, New York Life, is willing to commit substantial floating-rate capital to a well-located, amenitized office asset near Grand Central Terminal, even as the broader office sector continues to face scrutiny over demand and valuations. The refinancing also arrives on the heels of a significant tenant commitment, suggesting that leasing momentum and lender confidence are moving together for this specific property.

The loan carries a three-year initial term with two 12-month extension options, giving the sponsorship flexibility while the building's renovation program and leasing story continue to develop. The property spans 750,000 square feet and was built in 1926. The financing was arranged by an Estreich & Company team of Jonathan Estreich, Peter A. Duncan and Egor Petrov, along with a Newmark team of Adam Spies, Adam Doneger and Willis Robbins, according to George Comfort & Sons. The floating-rate structure is notable because it exposes the borrower to interest rate movements over the initial term, a choice that may reflect expectations about near-term rate conditions or a preference for flexibility over fixed-rate certainty.

The refinancing was closed after Havas Health signed a 15-year lease extension and expansion in August of its global headquarters at the building, growing from 189,461 square feet to 254,118 square feet, as Commercial Observer first reported at the time. Other office tenants include architecture practice Spectorgroup and law firm BraunHagey & Borden. Peter S. Duncan, president and CEO of George Comfort & Sons, said in a statement that the refinancing "underscores the lending community's confidence in office assets like 200 Madison Avenue that are defined by top-tier amenities, unrivaled location and strong sponsorship." He added that the transaction, combined with the ongoing renovation program and leasing momentum, positions the property for continued success.

The property recently underwent a lobby renovation and is in the process of adding 11,000 square feet of amenity space on the 10th floor, including executive conference rooms, a game area, a lounge and event space. Those capital improvements, along with the Havas Health expansion, provide concrete evidence of an ownership strategy aimed at retaining and attracting tenants in a competitive Midtown East market. The source also notes that PincusCo first reported the deal but had an incorrect loan amount, a reminder that early reports on complex financings can contain inaccuracies that are later corrected by fuller reporting.

The evidence base for this analysis is limited to a single Commercial Observer article read in full. New York Life, Loeb Partners Realty and Jamestown did not immediately return requests for comment, according to the report, so the lender's own rationale and the borrower's use of proceeds are not directly confirmed. The dossier does not include loan-to-value, debt yield, interest rate spread, or other underwriting metrics, and it does not state whether the refinancing retires existing debt or provides cash-out proceeds. Those unknowns mean the transaction should be read as a positive but narrow signal about one asset and one lender, rather than a broad read on the entire office financing market.

What to watch next is whether the extension options are exercised, whether additional tenants follow Havas Health in expanding, and whether other lenders cite this deal as a comparable for Midtown East office refinancings. The floating-rate structure also means the loan's performance will be sensitive to interest rate changes over the next three years. For now, the transaction is best understood as evidence that a 750,000-square-foot office building with recent leasing momentum, active capital improvements, and proximity to Grand Central Terminal can still attract a large institutional refinancing.
