HITT breaks ground on $500M solid rocket motor manufacturing site

HITT Contracting has begun construction on Avio USA's 900,000-square-foot solid rocket motor plant in Hurt, Virginia, a $500 million investment tied to tactical

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

HITT Contracting, the eighth largest contractor in the country by revenue, has broken ground on Avio USA's new 900,000-square-foot solid rocket motor plant in Hurt, Virginia. The facility represents a $500 million investment, according to a Sept. 30 announcement from Rep. John McGuire, R-Va. Avio USA, a subsidiary of Italian rocket and propulsion firm Avio S.p.A., will use the site to produce thousands of solid rocket motors annually for U.S. tactical missile programs, with clients and end users including Lockheed Martin and Raytheon. The groundbreaking matters because it adds another major node to a defense manufacturing buildout that contractors have described as one of the most concentrated since World War II.

The project's mechanics are substantial. HITT will act as general contractor for a build featuring more than 35 separate structures designed for protective construction, environmental controls, and administrative spaces. Josh Foreso, executive vice president at HITT, told Construction Dive via email that the site's complex topography poses construction logistics challenges but works well for the final layout and long-term functionality. Construction is expected to complete in 2028, with production beginning in early 2029. The timeline implies a multi-year execution window before the plant contributes to missile motor output.

The evidence comes from a single Construction Dive article read in full, which draws on HITT's announcement, Rep. McGuire's statement, and an email from Foreso. The article also cites Westside Construction Group, a commercial general contractor based in Churchville, New York, which in May pegged the solid rocket motor construction wave at more than $3 billion. The Defense Department's inspector general recently reported that the Iran war has led to a shortage of U.S. munitions, according to NBC News, providing context for why capacity expansion is occurring. No independent corroboration of the $500 million figure or the $3 billion wave estimate is available within the dossier.

For the commercial real estate and construction sector, the project signals durable demand in defense-aligned industrial work. HITT's role as general contractor on a 900,000-square-foot, multi-structure campus suggests contractors with protective construction and environmental controls expertise are positioned to capture similar awards. The broader wave of solid rocket motor projects, if the $3 billion estimate holds, could support a pipeline of specialized industrial builds beyond this single site. However, the dossier does not provide financing terms, margin expectations, or competitive bid details, so market implications remain directional rather than quantified.

Key limitations include the single-source nature of the reporting and the absence of Avio USA's own announcement or financial disclosures. The $500 million investment figure is attributed to a political representative rather than a corporate filing, and the $3 billion wave estimate comes from a contractor's May assessment, not an independent market study. What to watch includes whether Avio confirms the investment amount, whether additional solid rocket motor projects are announced, and whether the 2028 completion and 2029 production start dates hold given the site's described topographic challenges.
