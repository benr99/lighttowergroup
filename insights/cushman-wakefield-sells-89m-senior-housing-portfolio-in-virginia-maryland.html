Cushman & Wakefield Sells $89M Senior Housing Portfolio in Virginia, Maryland

A two-asset, 130-unit senior housing portfolio in Great Falls, Virginia and Queenstown, Maryland traded for $89 million, with LTC Properties acquiring the recen

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Cushman & Wakefield has arranged the $89 million sale of a two-asset senior housing portfolio totaling 130 units across Virginia and Maryland, a transaction that underscores continued investor appetite for modern, high-quality seniors housing in supply-constrained submarkets. The portfolio consists of two assisted living and memory care communities: The Residence at Colvin Run in Great Falls, Virginia, and Queenstown Landing in Queenstown, Maryland. The facilities were built in 2022 and 2021, respectively, and the portfolio was 94% occupied at closing, according to the announcement. The sale matters because it shows institutional capital moving into recently constructed senior housing assets with strong occupancy, even as broader commercial real estate transaction activity remains selective.

The seller group included Buvermo Investments, SHA Capital, Verity Commercial and INDIE. The buyer was LTC Properties, Inc., a real estate investment trust, which acquired the portfolio for $89 million. IntegraCare was retained as operator, suggesting continuity in day-to-day management despite the ownership change. The Cushman & Wakefield Senior Housing Capital Markets group advised the sellers. The team was led by Vice Chair Joshua Jandris, Vice Chair Brett Gardner, Director Robert Bracci and Senior Financial Analyst Evan Jakobsze. The announcement did not disclose financing terms, capitalization rates, per-unit pricing, or other valuation metrics.

The only source identified in the dossier is a Connect CRE article published on October 8, 2026. Connect CRE is a secondary trade publication focused on commercial real estate news, and the dossier classifies it as tier 2 authority. The article is short—approximately 1,412 characters—and the dossier notes that the evidence level is single full text, meaning the analysis must stay close to one source read in full. The key quantitative facts are the $89 million sale price, the 130-unit portfolio size, the 94% occupancy at closing, and the 2021 and 2022 construction dates. The quote attributed to Joshua Jandris frames the transaction as reflecting "continued demand for modern, high-quality seniors housing assets in tight markets with strong demographic fundamentals." No independent corroboration from public records, buyer or seller disclosures, or additional trade outlets is present in the dossier.

For the senior housing sector, the transaction carries several supported implications. First, the buyer is a REIT, LTC Properties, which typically seeks income-producing healthcare and seniors housing assets; the 94% occupancy and recent construction align with that profile. Second, the retention of IntegraCare as operator indicates that the buyer valued operational continuity rather than bringing in a new management platform. Third, the portfolio's location in Great Falls, Virginia and Queenstown, Maryland points to affluent or demographically favorable submarkets, though the source does not provide household income, age cohort, or supply data to quantify that claim. The sale also establishes LTC Properties' presence in two submarkets, as stated by Jandris, but the source does not say whether LTC already owned other assets in those areas or how the acquisition fits into a broader portfolio strategy.

The main limitation is the thinness of the evidence base. The dossier contains no information on the properties' square footage, unit mix, revenue, net operating income, or historical performance. It also does not identify the brokers' compensation, the marketing process, the number of bidders, or whether the sale was marketed broadly or negotiated off-market. The absence of per-unit pricing—$89 million divided by 130 units implies roughly $684,600 per unit, but that calculation is an inference, not a stated fact—means the transaction cannot be benchmarked against other senior housing sales without additional data. Furthermore, the source does not explain why the seller group chose to divest, whether the sale was part of a fund liquidation or portfolio rebalancing, or what the buyer's expected yield or hold period might be.

What to watch next is whether LTC Properties or the seller group discloses additional terms in earnings releases or regulatory filings, and whether other senior housing portfolios in the Mid-Atlantic trade at comparable occupancy and construction vintage. If subsequent reporting confirms the per-unit price or cap rate, the transaction could serve as a data point for pricing recently built assisted living and memory care assets. Until then, the $89 million sale should be read as a single, source-limited signal of demand rather than a broad market benchmark.
