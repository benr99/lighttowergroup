Bnos Chaya Efraim Foundation Pays $28M for 111,756-SF Borough Park Building With Bank of Princeton Loan

Backed by a $21 million loan from The Bank of Princeton, the Borough Park closing clears at $251 a square foot across 111,756 square feet.

In Borough Park, where growing community schools and foundations compete fiercely for large-floorplate masonry buildings, a 111,756-square-foot pre-war warehouse on 38th Street is an irreplaceable campus prize. The Bnos Chaya Efraim Foundation paid $28 million ($251 per square foot) on September 4, 2026 to acquire the four-story building at 1365 38th Street from 1365 Realty Corp., financing the purchase with a $21 million senior mortgage from The Bank of Princeton.

Across the property's 111,756 square feet on a 24,974-square-foot M1-2 lot, the $28 million trade establishes a $251 a square foot clearing mark for Borough Park 4-Story Institutional & Industrial Building product.

The Bank of Princeton Provides 75 Percent Leverage for a Borough Park Campus

While conventional commercial lenders rarely cross 60 percent loan-to-value on 1915-vintage industrial conversions, New Jersey-based relationship lender The Bank of Princeton wrote a $21 million senior mortgage—exactly 75 percent of the $28 million purchase price—backed by $7 million of foundation equity.

Spanning nearly 28,000 square feet per floor across four stories on a 24,974-square-foot lot between 13th and 14th avenues, 1365 38th Street offers the high ceilings and column spacing needed for a flagship educational and community center at just $251 a square foot—less than a third of ground-up institutional construction costs.

From Our Desk

On our placement desk at Light Tower Group, 1365 38th Street is a live case study in how Brooklyn deals actually cross the finish line right now. Nonprofit educational and community buyers remain the ultimate price-makers for heavy-floorplate M1-2 buildings in central Brooklyn, and regional banks like The Bank of Princeton will gladly stretch to 75 percent LTV when backed by entrenched community fundraising.
