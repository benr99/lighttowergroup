Historic NoHo Loft Building at 9 Great Jones Street Sells for $15.25M—Six Times Its 2004 Price

Backed by a $8.2 million loan from Peapack Private Bank & Trust, the NoHo closing clears at $915 a square foot across 16,660 square feet.

On Great Jones Street—the two-block cobblestone-era enclave linking Broadway and the Bowery in historic NoHo—1890s loft buildings only surface when a twenty-year owner decides to cash out. 9 Great Jones PO L.P. closed on the six-story, 16,660-square-foot loft building at 9 Great Jones Street on August 21, 2026 for $15.25 million ($915 per square foot), rewarding seller Bong, LLC with a 500.4 percent gain over the $2.54 million price paid in January 2004.

The per-foot math explains why Bong, LLC chose this moment to harvest gains—and why 9 Great Jones PO L.P. was willing to pay up. At $915 a square foot (or $2,541,667 per apartment across 5 residential units) across 16,660 square feet, the closing price represents a 500.4 percent appreciation over the $2.54 million trade recorded in January 2004. Based on underwritten net operating income, the trade clears at a 5.80 percent going-in cap rate.

A 6x Generational Gain Financed by Peapack Private Bank's $8.2M CEMA

To finance the acquisition, Peapack Private Bank & Trust originated an $8.2 million Mortgage and Consolidation (M&CON;) loan—representing 53.8 percent loan-to-value—while 9 Great Jones PO L.P. wrote a $7.05 million cash equity check.

Consolidating the existing debt via an M&CON; CEMA saved the buyer roughly $229,600 in mortgage recording taxes at closing. On a block where boutique full-floor condominiums next door trade above $2,500 a square foot, acquiring a six-story 1890 loft building at $915 a square foot and a 5.80 percent cap rate is a textbook downtown trophy play.
