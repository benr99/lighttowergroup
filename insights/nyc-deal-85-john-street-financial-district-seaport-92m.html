Brookfield Asset Management and Metro Loft (Nathan Berman) Takes Down Financial District / Seaport's 85 John Street for $92M at $500 a Foot

Backed by a $59.8 million loan from Freddie Mac, the Financial District / Seaport trade clears at $500 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

Long before office-to-residential conversion became a city hall talking point, Lower Manhattan's insurance buildings around John and Gold Streets proved that 1920s masonry towers make phenomenal apartment buildings. Brookfield and conversion pioneer Nathan Berman's Metro Loft paid $92 million ($500 a square foot, or $464,646 per unit) to acquire the 198-unit rental at 85 John Street from AXA IM Alts. When the deed closed on February 1, 2026, Brookfield Asset Management and Metro Loft (Nathan Berman) had officially taken down 85 John Street from AXA IM Alts for $92 million, with Newmark brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 184,000-square-foot building last traded in April 2017 for $104 million. Stepping in at $500 a square foot—or $464,646 a door across its 198 apartments— shaves 11.5 percent off that prior valuation, giving Brookfield Asset Management and Metro Loft (Nathan Berman) a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle. Even better for the buyers, the in-place rent roll hands them a verified 5.70 percent going-in cap rate from day one.

Freddie Mac + CEMA: The $59.8 Million Formula

How you structure the debt on a Manhattan acquisition matters almost as much as what you pay for the deed. Freddie Mac (via Newmark) stepped up with a $59.8 million senior loan—representing 65 percent loan-to-value—while the buyers wrote a $32.2 million equity check behind it.

Rather than extinguishing the seller's old note and originating a scratch mortgage, the buyers and Freddie Mac kept the existing mortgage wrapper alive through a Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement, or CEMA. Because New York City levies a roughly 2.8 percent mortgage recording tax on new commercial debt, that single piece of legal plumbing saved the deal upwards of $1.67 million at the closing table—cash that stays in the property's capex and leasing reserve instead of going to the Department of Finance.

From Our Desk

When we look at 85 John Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and lender selection. Brookfield Asset Management and Metro Loft (Nathan Berman) didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Financial District / Seaport footage at $500 a square foot and paired it with the exact lender bucket built for this business plan. Buying proven Lower Manhattan residential conversions at $500 a foot—11.5 percent below their 2017 mark—while rolling the seller's mortgage via CEMA into a 65 percent Freddie Mac loan is as clean as core-plus real estate gets.
