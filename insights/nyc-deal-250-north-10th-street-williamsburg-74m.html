Ponte Gadea (Amancio Ortega's Family Office) and Clipper Equity Takes Down Williamsburg's 250 North 10th Street for $74M at $385 a Foot

Backed by a $44.4 million loan from Freddie Mac, the Williamsburg trade clears at $385 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

Two blocks north of the Bedford Avenue L train, where former brick factories now house $5,200-a-month one-bedrooms, Nuveen Real Estate decided it was time to rotate out of its 234-unit Williamsburg flagship. The buyers emerged with a global-meets-local pedigree: Spanish billionaire Amancio Ortega's Ponte Gadea teamed up with Brooklyn veteran Clipper Equity to take down the full-block rental at $316,239 a door—a 10.3 percent discount to Nuveen's 2016 check. When the deed closed on March 11, 2026, Ponte Gadea (Amancio Ortega's Family Office) and Clipper Equity had officially taken down 250 North 10th Street from Nuveen Real Estate for $74 million, with Eastdil Secured brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 192,000-square-foot building last traded in May 2016 for $82.5 million. Stepping in at $385 a square foot—or $316,239 a door across its 234 apartments— shaves 10.3 percent off that prior valuation, giving Ponte Gadea (Amancio Ortega's Family Office) and Clipper Equity a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle. Even better for the buyers, the in-place rent roll hands them a verified 5.80 percent going-in cap rate from day one.

Freddie Mac Steps In at 60 Percent Leverage

The real tell in any Brooklyn closing sits on the debt side of the ledger. To fund the purchase, Ponte Gadea (Amancio Ortega's Family Office) and Clipper Equity locked in a $44.4 million 7-year fixed freddie mac optigo loan from Freddie Mac (via Walker & Dunlop) at 60 percent loan-to-value.

That loan commitment meant the buyers only needed to write a $29.6 million equity check to control a 6-story, 2015-vintage Williamsburg asset. Credit committees aren't stretching to 75 percent leverage anymore, but when a sponsor brings $29.6 million of real cash equity and a basis of $385 a square foot, lenders like Freddie Mac are wide open for business.

From Our Desk

When we look at 250 North 10th Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and lender selection. Ponte Gadea (Amancio Ortega's Family Office) and Clipper Equity didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Williamsburg footage at $385 a square foot and paired it with the exact lender bucket built for this business plan. When free-market Williamsburg rentals trade 10 percent below their 2016 peak despite 35 percent cumulative rent growth since then, buyers locking in 60 percent Freddie Mac paper are capturing the best risk-adjusted yield north of Atlantic Avenue.
