Burlington Moving HQ To Philly, Buys Office Building For $240M

Discount clothing retailer Burlington is moving its corporate headquarters from Burlington, New Jersey, to Philadelphia's Schuylkill Yards, purchasing a 441K SF office building at 3151 Market St. for $240M. The relocation, announced Thursday, will bring 2,000 workers and $370M worth of investment to University City over the next five years, according to a press release from the governor's office. Gov. Josh Shapiro called it "the largest headquarters relocation to the city in years," underscoring the symbolic and economic weight of a company with deep South Jersey roots choosing Philadelphia as its new home base.

The transaction mechanics are unusually concrete for a headquarters move. Burlington bought the building from Brandywine Realty Trust for $240M, equating to $544 per SF, according to a document the REIT filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission. The property is part of Brandywine's Schuylkill Yards development and was completed last year. At the time the deal was finalized Monday, the building was only 4% leased, according to the SEC filing. The deal is expected to close by the end of September. Brandywine will use the proceeds to pay off a $57.3M loan and expects to net $168M from the sale.

The public-sector support is a central part of the story. Burlington received a $7M forgivable loan and a job creation tax credit award from the city to support the move. SEPTA and the Philadelphia Parking Authority are also offering discounts to commuters working at the new headquarters. These incentives, combined with the building's location in a development marketed as a place for "world-class talent" and collaboration, help explain why a company founded in 1972 and based in its namesake New Jersey township would relocate across the Delaware River. Brandywine CEO Jerry Sweeney said Burlington's investment "validates our vision for Schuylkill Yards as a place where companies can access world-class talent, foster collaboration, and accelerate growth."

The deal has broader implications for Philadelphia's office market, particularly University City. The University City office market had a 12.8% vacancy rate last quarter, lower than the 18.9% and 17.9% seen in Market East and Market West, respectively, according to Colliers. The 441K SF of office space Burlington bought is equivalent to more than 10% of the 4.3M SF of inventory the brokerage counted in University City at the time. That scale means the transaction could meaningfully tighten the submarket's vacancy rate once the headquarters is occupied, while also validating the appeal of newer, well-located office product in a broader market still grappling with elevated vacancy.

The evidence base is limited to a single full-text source, Bisnow Philadelphia, which draws on an SEC filing, a governor's office press release, and Colliers data. Several details remain unconfirmed by independent reporting: the exact structure and timing of the job creation tax credit, the terms of the forgivable loan beyond its $7M face value, and the specific discounts offered by SEPTA and the Philadelphia Parking Authority. The $370M investment figure comes from the governor's office and has not been independently corroborated. What to watch next is whether the deal closes by the end of September as expected, how quickly Burlington occupies the building, and whether the relocation prompts other New Jersey-based companies to consider similar cross-river moves.
