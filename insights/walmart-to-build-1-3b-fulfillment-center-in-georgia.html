Walmart to build $1.3B fulfillment center in Georgia

A 1.5-million-square-foot automated facility in Carnesville is the latest expansion of Walmart's next-generation fulfillment network, with construction slated t

Ben Rohr September 01, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Walmart plans to build a 1.5-million-square-foot automated fulfillment center in Carnesville, Georgia, according to an Aug. 27 press release from the Georgia Department of Economic Development. The high-tech, next-generation facility represents a $1.3 billion investment and will create 1,000 jobs, per the release. The announcement matters because it extends Walmart's strategy of placing automated fulfillment capacity closer to customers to expand same-day and next-day shipping capabilities across the U.S., while also deepening the retailer's physical and logistical footprint in a state where it already operates 209 Walmart stores and Sam's Club locations and 11 supply chain facilities.

The material facts are limited but specific. The facility is planned for Carnesville, Georgia, and construction is slated to start in late 2026. The source does not disclose a completion date, the facility's expected throughput, the mix of goods it will handle, or the wage and skill profile of the 1,000 announced jobs. The $1.3 billion figure is presented as the total investment, but the dossier does not break that amount into land, construction, automation equipment, or other categories. The 1.5-million-square-foot size and the description as a "high-tech, next-generation fulfillment center" indicate a substantial automated operation, but no additional operational metrics are provided in the available evidence.

The evidence comes from a single secondary source, Construction Dive, which summarizes a press release from the Georgia Department of Economic Development. The dossier notes that the source was read in full and that the analysis must stay close to it. The press release is the underlying authority for the investment amount, job count, square footage, and construction timeline. The source also provides context on Walmart's broader fulfillment strategy: the retailer first outlined plans for high-tech facilities in 2022, saying it would build four next-generation fulfillment centers over three years, and in 2023 announced a fifth facility set to open in California this year to reach more of the West Coast with next- or two-day shipping. Because the dossier contains only one full-text source, the claims are not independently corroborated within the available evidence.

The sector implications point to continued investment in automated logistics and commercial real estate tied to e-commerce fulfillment. The source states that automation remains a strategic priority for Walmart, which plans to retrofit 23 of its 42 regional distribution centers with automation and intends to eventually upgrade all locations. As of 2025, more than half of Walmart's e-commerce fulfillment center volume moved through automated systems. The Georgia project fits a pattern of capital spending across Walmart's supply chain, with spending expected to peak over the course of this year and 2027. For the commercial real estate sector, the announcement signals demand for large-footprint industrial facilities in the Southeast, though the dossier does not provide comparable market data or details on incentives, site selection criteria, or local infrastructure.

The main limitation is the thin evidence base. The analysis relies on one secondary source summarizing a government press release, and several material details are missing: the facility's completion date, the nature of the 1,000 jobs, the specific automation systems to be installed, and any public incentives or local approvals. The dossier also does not include Walmart's own statement or financial filings, so the investment figure and timeline should be treated as announced plans rather than completed commitments. What to watch is whether construction begins on schedule in late 2026, whether Walmart discloses additional operational details, and how the Carnesville facility compares with the retailer's other next-generation fulfillment centers in terms of capacity, automation, and regional shipping coverage.
