Newmark Arranges $215.8M Bridge Refinancing for Five-Property Florida Multifamily Portfolio

Newmark secured $215.8 million in construction takeout bridge financing through Benefit Street Partners for Waypoint Residential's 1,274-unit Florida portfolio,

Ben Rohr September 03, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Newmark arranged $215.8 million in bridge refinancing for Waypoint Residential's five-property, 1,274-unit multifamily portfolio spanning key markets throughout Florida. The transaction matters because it represents a construction takeout bridge financing—a mechanism that replaces short-term construction debt with interim financing—allowing the sponsor to stabilize newly delivered assets before pursuing permanent debt. The deal signals continued lender appetite for recently completed Florida multifamily product, even as the broader capital markets environment remains selective.

The financing was secured by Newmark's Matthew Williams and Rob Wright through Benefit Street Partners, with support from James Maynard and Kyle Schlitt. The portfolio comprises five newly constructed, luxury garden-style multifamily communities located in Vero Beach, Port St. Lucie, Palm Bay, Davenport, and Gainesville. Three properties are situated along Florida's Treasure Coast and Space Coast and were completed in 2024. The remaining two assets include The Bradley at Lake Wilson in Davenport and The Marlow Gainesville, located near the University of Florida and UF Health.

The source reporting comes from Connect CRE, a secondary trade publication covering commercial real estate, with the article published on September 3, 2026. The dossier contains a single full-text source, and the analysis must remain close to that evidence. The reported facts include the loan amount of $215.8 million, the portfolio size of 1,274 units, the five-property composition, the lender identity, and the Newmark team members involved. No additional terms—such as interest rate, loan-to-value ratio, maturity, or recourse structure—were disclosed in the source material.

The market context cited in the source points to specific demand drivers for the three Treasure Coast and Space Coast properties: continued population and employment growth, a moderating new supply pipeline, and proximity to major healthcare, aerospace, advanced manufacturing, and logistics employers. The Bradley at Lake Wilson benefits from access to Orlando's employment centers, tourism corridor, and major transportation infrastructure. The Marlow Gainesville is positioned near the University of Florida and UF Health, described as two of the area's largest economic drivers. These locational attributes suggest the portfolio is anchored by employment and institutional demand rather than purely speculative growth.
