Craig Deitelzweig's Marx Realty and Merchants Hospitality Snaps Up 545 Madison Avenue for $79.5M in a 24% Plaza District Basis Reset

Backed by a $54.5 million loan from Peachtree Group Credit, the Plaza District trade clears at $569 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

Craig Deitelzweig has spent the past five years proving a simple contrarian thesis: Manhattan office tenants will still pay top dollar if you make the lobby feel like a private club in Mayfair rather than a corporate waiting room. Now the Marx Realty chief is bringing that hospitality playbook to the corner of Madison Avenue and East 55th Street, buying the 18-story glass-curtain-wall tower at a 24.3 percent discount to its $105 million 2019 valuation. When the deed closed on March 18, 2026, Craig Deitelzweig's Marx Realty and Merchants Hospitality had officially taken down 545 Madison Avenue from LCOR Incorporated for $79.5 million, with Eastdil Secured brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 139,600-square-foot building last traded in June 2019 for $105 million. Stepping in at $569 a square foot shaves 24.3 percent off that prior valuation, giving Craig Deitelzweig's Marx Realty and Merchants Hospitality a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle.

The CEMA Trick That Saved Seven Figures at the Closing Table

How you structure the debt on a Manhattan acquisition matters almost as much as what you pay for the deed. Peachtree Group Credit stepped up with a $54.5 million senior loan—representing 68.6 percent loan-to-value—while the buyers wrote a $25 million equity check behind it.

Rather than extinguishing the seller's old note and originating a scratch mortgage, the buyers and Peachtree Group Credit kept the existing mortgage wrapper alive through a Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement, or CEMA. Because New York City levies a roughly 2.8 percent mortgage recording tax on new commercial debt, that single piece of legal plumbing saved the deal upwards of $1.53 million at the closing table—cash that stays in the property's capex and leasing reserve instead of going to the Department of Finance.

From Our Desk

When we look at 545 Madison Avenue on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and lender selection. Craig Deitelzweig's Marx Realty and Merchants Hospitality didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Plaza District footage at $569 a square foot and paired it with the exact lender bucket built for this business plan. Private credit shops aren't allergic to Midtown office—they were simply waiting for the basis to reset below $600 a foot and for borrowers sharp enough to protect day-one cash with a CEMA tax shield.
