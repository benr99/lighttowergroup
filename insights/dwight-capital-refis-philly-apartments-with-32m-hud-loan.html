Dwight Capital Refis Philly Apartments With $32M HUD Loan

Scannapieco Development has landed $32 million of agency-backed debt to refinance the 110-unit Americana in Philadelphia's Kensington neighborhood, according to

Ben Rohr October 05, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Scannapieco Development has secured $32 million of agency-backed debt to refinance the Americana, a newly built 110-unit multifamily property in Philadelphia's Kensington neighborhood, Commercial Observer reported on October 5, 2026. The transaction matters because it shows a HUD 223(f) loan being used to recapitalize a recently completed rental project, converting construction-era capital into long-term agency debt while giving the developer access to equity built up since the building debuted in 2024. For a market still recalibrating after higher-rate disruption, the deal is a small but concrete signal that agency execution remains available for stabilized, newly constructed urban infill rental assets.

The loan was closed by Dwight Capital under the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development's 223(f) program. According to Dwight, proceeds will fund a replacement reserve for future capital improvements and provide Scannapieco with access to equity accumulated since construction. The transaction was originated by Dwight's Jonathan Pomper and Jack Tawil. The property is located at 1775 North American Street, roughly three miles north of Philadelphia's Center City area. The five-story building includes ground-floor retail space leased to The Learning Experience and Allie Lasure Fitness, while resident amenities include a clubhouse, an 8,000-square-foot pool deck, an outdoor lounge with grilling areas, a game room, and a two-level fitness center.

The factual record is limited to a single Commercial Observer article read in full. The report does not disclose the loan's interest rate, term, amortization schedule, debt yield, loan-to-value ratio, or the amount of equity being returned to the sponsor. It also does not state the original construction financing amount or the property's current occupancy, rent roll, or operating income. The only named counterparties are Scannapieco Development, Dwight Capital, and HUD, and the only named originators are Jonathan Pomper and Jack Tawil. Philadelphia-based Scannapieco did not immediately return a request for comment, according to the source, so the borrower's own characterization of the refinancing is absent from the record.

The transaction's sector implications are modest but directionally useful. HUD 223(f) loans are typically long-term, fixed-rate, fully amortizing instruments used for stabilized properties, and their use here suggests the Americana has reached a level of operational maturity sufficient to support agency underwriting less than two years after completion. The presence of two ground-floor retail tenants, including a childcare operator and a fitness business, indicates the project was underwritten with a mixed-use income stream rather than purely residential rents. The Kensington location, three miles north of Center City, places the asset in a submarket that has seen uneven investment interest, making the agency refinancing a potentially notable vote of confidence in the property's performance, though the source does not provide submarket comparables or rent benchmarks to confirm that interpretation.

The main limitation is the absence of corroborating documentation. No HUD closing statement, servicer data, property record, or borrower disclosure is cited, and the article's key facts—loan amount, unit count, completion year, and address—are drawn from a single secondary trade publication. The report also contains a typographical rendering of the originator's name as "Dwght" in one sentence, a minor but notable quality issue in an otherwise straightforward account. What to watch next includes any public filing or servicer data confirming the loan amount and terms, any statement from Scannapieco on how the equity proceeds will be redeployed, and whether additional Kensington multifamily assets pursue similar agency refinancings, which would indicate broader lender appetite for the submarket.
