CoStar Pays $800M Cash for Zonda's New-Home Data Engine

At 4.7 times 2025 revenue, CoStar's new-home data expansion is a cash bet on lot-level information.

Ben Rohr August 22, 2026 2 min read 1 source

CoStar paid $800 million in cash for Zonda, which produced about $170 million in revenue in 2025. That equals roughly 4.7 times 2025 revenue.

CoStar Group said on August 21 that it had completed the acquisition first announced in May. Zonda brings proprietary lot-level data on new-home communities, land development, construction activity, sales and builder operations, serving more than 3,000 homebuilders, developers, lenders and suppliers. The announcement frames the move as an entry into the roughly $400 billion annual U.S. new-home sales market. Andy Florance, founder and CEO of CoStar, said the market needs "purpose-built information, analytics and marketing solutions."

Why now? CoStar's residential business is growing faster than the overall company. In the second quarter of 2026, total revenue rose 18 percent year over year to $925 million. Residential revenue rose 33 percent to $444 million. New-home construction data and marketplaces extend that segment into the new-home sales market.

The deal also hands CoStar two consumer-facing marketplaces, NewHomeSource.com and Livabl. That is the visible layer. The infrastructure layer is more interesting: CoStar said it plans to explore combining Zonda's Envision visualization platform with Matterport's spatial technology. The company said the combination could create richer digital home-shopping experiences.

The rationale is not hard to see: this was a build-versus-buy choice. Zonda's lot-level data is built through years of builder relationships and field collection. CoStar did not describe an equivalent in-house asset in its announcement. Zonda had that. CoStar could either build it slowly or buy it now with cash.

Zonda's president, Jeff Meyers, said joining CoStar gives Zonda "scale, technology and resources" to accelerate innovation. That is a seller's explanation, but it also names the constraint: a smaller platform with roughly $170 million in revenue may need CoStar's capital and distribution to grow into the $400 billion market it tracks.

What remains unknown is profitability. CoStar did not disclose Zonda's margins, EBITDA, or whether the $800 million included debt or other adjustments. The revenue multiple is clear from the disclosed figures. The earnings multiple is not.

For CRE owners and lenders, the takeaway is narrower than a consumer-tech story. The announcement focuses on builders, developers, lenders, investors and suppliers as the users of Zonda's data. New-home lot-level information could extend CoStar's coverage toward builder finance, land development lending, and residential supply tracking. The test is not whether CoStar can close the deal. It already did. The test is whether it turns Zonda's data into products CRE lenders will pay for.
