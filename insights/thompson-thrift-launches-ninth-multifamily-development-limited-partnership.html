Thompson Thrift launches ninth multifamily development limited partnership

Thompson Thrift has launched its ninth multi-project development partnership, seeking $200 million to $230 million to develop six class A multifamily communitie

Ben Rohr September 24, 2026 3 min read 2 sources
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Thompson Thrift, a full-service, nationally recognized real estate company and multifamily developer, has launched Thompson Thrift 2027 Multifamily Development, LP, its ninth multi-project development partnership. The offering seeks to raise approximately $200 million to $230 million in total capital commitments from accredited equity partners. The capital is earmarked to develop a geographically diversified portfolio of class A multifamily communities. The launch matters because it signals that a major multifamily developer sees a turning point in the development cycle after what company leaders describe as four years of correction, and because it gives accredited investors a single-commitment vehicle to gain exposure to multiple projects rather than underwriting individual deals.

The partnership has identified six development opportunities across Colorado, Kentucky, Montana, Arizona and Nevada. The portfolio reflects Thompson Thrift's continued expansion across the western United States and includes its first multifamily development opportunity in Montana. The limited partnership is being offered exclusively to accredited investors pursuant to Regulation D, Rule 506(c) under the Securities Act of 1933. Because general solicitation is permitted under Rule 506(c), purchasers must meet verification requirements to validate their accredited investor status. The structure is consistent with Thompson Thrift's prior multi-project development partnerships, though the dossier does not provide performance data for the earlier eight vehicles.

The announcement is corroborated across two independent secondary sources: Institutional Real Estate and Yield PRO. Yield PRO provides the fuller record, including direct quotes from company leadership. CEO and co-founder Paul Thrift said that after four years of correction, new starts have been declining for several years, the record wave of new supply has largely been absorbed, and the company is beginning to see improvement in rents and concessions alongside continued strong demand and healthy occupancy. Josh Purvis, managing partner of Thompson Thrift Residential, said the six identified opportunities reflect the company's ability to move in and out of markets and deploy capital where fundamentals create the greatest opportunity. The company also reported that since 2010 it has started more than 27,000 residential units and deployed approximately $2 billion in equity capital, and that since its founding it has invested more than $7.3 billion into local communities. Those historical figures appear in only one source and are not independently corroborated in the dossier.

The launch has implications for the multifamily development sector. A developer of Thompson Thrift's scale raising fresh equity for new class A construction suggests that at least some well-capitalized operators believe the supply overhang from the recent building wave is clearing and that rent and concession trends are improving. The geographic mix, spanning Mountain West and Sun Belt markets, indicates where the company sees the most favorable supply-and-demand dynamics. For accredited investors, the vehicle offers diversified exposure through a single commitment, which can reduce single-project concentration risk but also concentrates manager risk in Thompson Thrift's site selection and execution.

Several limitations temper the signal. The dossier does not include the target fund size relative to prior partnerships, the projected number of units, development timelines, projected returns, or the equity partners involved. The $7.3 billion and $2 billion historical figures are uncorroborated across sources. The company's statements about market conditions are forward-looking and reflect management's view, not independent market data. What to watch is whether the partnership reaches its $200 million to $230 million target, whether the Montana project proceeds as the company's first in that state, and whether rent and concession improvements cited by management are confirmed by third-party multifamily data over the next several quarters.
