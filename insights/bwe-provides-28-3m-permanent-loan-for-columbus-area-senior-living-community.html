BWE Provides $28.3M Permanent Loan for Columbus-Area Senior Living Community

BWE has originated a $28.3 million Fannie Mae permanent loan for Grove City Senior Living, a 124-unit independent living, assisted living and memory care commun

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

BWE has provided a $28.3 million permanent loan for Grove City Senior Living, an independent living, assisted living and memory care community in Grove City, part of the Columbus metropolitan area. The financing matters because it converts a recently completed development's construction debt into a long-term capital structure, allowing the Ohio-based developer to return equity to investors while positioning for a long-term hold. The property opened in May 2023, and the loan was originated by Taylor Hawkins, senior vice president in BWE's Cleveland office, through Fannie Mae.

The loan features a 10-year term with three years of interest-only payments and a 30-year amortization, as well as a 100 percent loan-to-cost ratio. The proceeds will be used to take out existing construction debt and return equity to investors. BWE is a Fannie Mae Delegated Underwriting and Servicing (DUS)-approved lender, which allows it to underwrite and service loans directly under the agency program. The property is located at 3615 Glacial Lane in Grove City and contains 124 units in studio, one- and two-bedroom arrangements across its independent living, assisted living and memory care divisions.

The only source for this transaction is a single article from RE Business Online, a secondary trade publication. The report includes a direct quote from Hawkins explaining the borrower's timing: "When Grove City Senior Living reached full occupancy, our client knew it was the right time to move on from its existing construction debt and find a permanent loan." Hawkins also noted that BWE considered multiple agency options and that "although Fannie Mae's senior book is shrinking, the strong fundamentals of Grove City Senior Living made it an attractive property [for lending]." The source does not disclose the borrower's name, the original construction loan amount, the property's occupancy rate, or the specific interest rate secured.

The transaction highlights how stabilized senior living assets in secondary Midwestern markets can still access agency permanent financing even as Fannie Mae's senior housing portfolio contracts. The 100 percent loan-to-cost ratio and interest-only period suggest strong lender confidence in the property's cash flow, supported by full occupancy and its location within the master-planned Beulah Park community. Residents have access to amenities including a library, billiards room, fitness room, spa, personalized support plans, emergency call systems, and personal transportation. The property's integration with Beulah Park gives residents access to multiple parks and historic downtown Grove City, which may support long-term demand.

Several limitations apply to this analysis. The dossier contains a single source with no corroborating documentation, and key financial details such as the interest rate, debt service coverage, and borrower identity are not disclosed. The claim of full occupancy comes from a quote by the originating lender, not from independent verification. The reference to Fannie Mae's shrinking senior book is also sourced only from Hawkins's statement. What to watch: whether the borrower's long-term hold strategy holds up if occupancy softens, how the 100 percent loan-to-cost ratio performs in a rising-rate or declining-valuation environment, and whether additional reporting confirms the transaction terms or reveals the developer behind the project.
