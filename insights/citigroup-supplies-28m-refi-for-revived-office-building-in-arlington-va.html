Citigroup Supplies $28M Refi for Revived Office Building in Arlington, Va.

A $28 million CMBS loan from Citigroup refinances Jemal Equities' 1530 Wilson Boulevard, where occupancy jumped from 61 percent to 89 percent in 12 months.

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Jemal Equities has secured a $28 million commercial mortgage-backed securities loan from Citigroup to refinance its 10-story office property at 1530 Wilson Boulevard in Arlington, Virginia's Rosslyn neighborhood. The transaction matters because it provides fresh capital to continue a turnaround that has already produced a sharp occupancy gain at a building that traded at a steep discount just one year earlier. The refinancing also signals that at least one major lender is willing to underwrite office assets with demonstrated leasing momentum, even in a market where office debt remains difficult to place.

The property spans roughly 174,000 square feet and was built in 1990. Jemal Equities, run by Douglas Development Managing Principal Matthew Jemal, acquired the building in October 2025 for $28 million from Velocis, when it was 61 percent leased. The building is now 89 percent leased, according to Meridian Capital Group, which arranged the loan. The weighted average lease term has also improved, rising from 2.8 years at the time of purchase to 6.8 years today. Recent capital improvements include a renovated lobby, an outdoor terrace, and a fitness center with locker rooms, with a 50-person conference center still under development.

The evidence comes from a single Commercial Observer report published October 7, 2026, which cites Meridian Capital Group as the source for the occupancy and lease-term figures. Meridian's Cary Pollack and Judah Neuman negotiated the debt. Pollack said in a statement that Jemal "has built a reputation for turning buildings around, and 1530 Wilson is the latest example," adding that Citi "recognized the strength of that execution." The report also notes that the $28 million acquisition price marked a 61 percent drop from the $71.4 million Velocis paid for the building in April 2020. Anchor tenants include Lincoln Property Company, Blue Origin, and SAS Institute. Citigroup and Jemal Equities did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The transaction illustrates a broader pattern in office real estate: deeply discounted acquisitions paired with aggressive leasing and capital improvements can create refinancing opportunities relatively quickly. The jump from 61 percent to 89 percent occupancy in under a year is the central evidence of that execution. The longer weighted average lease term of 6.8 years is also significant because it provides lenders with more predictable cash flow than the 2.8-year WALT at acquisition. Still, the report does not disclose the loan's interest rate, term, loan-to-value ratio, or debt yield, so the pricing and risk profile of the CMBS loan remain unknown.

Several limitations apply to this analysis. The dossier contains only one source, and key details such as the exact refinancing proceeds relative to the acquisition price, the building's current net operating income, and the identity of any new tenants beyond the named anchors are not provided. It is also unclear whether the occupancy gain reflects new leases signed by Jemal Equities or leases that were already in negotiation before the acquisition. What to watch next includes whether the conference center completion drives additional leasing, whether the 89 percent occupancy holds through any tenant rollover, and whether other lenders follow Citigroup's lead in financing turnaround office assets in Northern Virginia.
