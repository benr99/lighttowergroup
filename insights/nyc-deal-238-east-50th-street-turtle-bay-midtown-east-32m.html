A $32.5M Walk-Up Assemblage Quietly Comes Together on East 50th Street in Turtle Bay

Backed by a $7.06 million loan from Citizens Bank, N.A., the Turtle Bay / Midtown East closing clears at $751 a square foot across 43,254 square feet.

Along the tree-lined stretch of East 50th Street between Second and Third avenues, stitching together contiguous brownstone and tenement frontage usually takes a generation of patience and three separate probate attorneys. On September 23, 2026, 234-42 E 50 Street LLC closed on the three-parcel Turtle Bay walk-up assemblage anchored by 238 East 50th Street for $32.5 million, taking out F Astroid Realty LLC at more than triple the $9.88 million basis recorded on the lead parcel in late 2015.

The per-foot math explains why F Astroid Realty LLC chose this moment to harvest gains—and why 234-42 E 50 Street LLC was willing to pay up. At $751 a square foot (or $601,852 per apartment across 54 residential units) across 43,254 square feet, the closing price represents a 229.1 percent appreciation over the $9.88 million trade recorded in December 2015.

Low-Leverage Citizens Bank Debt and Contiguous Midtown East Scale

What stands out in the City Register filings is how little leverage the buyers placed on the assemblage at closing. Citizens Bank, N.A. originated a $7.06 million senior acquisition mortgage—just 21.7 percent of the $32.5 million purchase price—while the sponsorship wired $25.44 million in cash equity.

In an R8B contextual residential zone just blocks from the East Midtown office core and the 51st Street subway stop, controlling 11,298 square feet of contiguous lot area gives the buyer two ways to win: renovate and push free-market walk-up rents at a $601,852-per-door basis, or hold the low-levered assemblage for a boutique luxury project without floating-rate debt breathing down its neck.

From Our Desk

On our placement desk at Light Tower Group, 238 East 50th Street is a live case study in how Manhattan deals actually cross the finish line right now. By keeping Citizens Bank's senior note to just $7.06 million—under 22 percent of the purchase price—and writing a $25.44 million equity check, the buyers insulated a rare contiguous Turtle Bay footprint from carry stress while preserving every option for luxury residential repositioning.
