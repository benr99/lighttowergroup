JV Pays $220M for Alpharetta Hotel

Endeavor Real Estate Group and Cox acquired The Hotel at Avalon from Crescent Real Estate for $220 million, according to Connect CRE.

Ben Rohr September 29, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Crescent Real Estate has sold The Hotel at Avalon, a 330-key hotel in Alpharetta, Georgia, to a joint venture of Endeavor Real Estate Group and Cox for $220 million, according to Connect CRE. The transaction matters because it represents a sizable single-asset hospitality trade in a prominent Atlanta-area mixed-use district, and it shifts ownership of a property that Crescent had held only since September 2020. The reported price also provides a concrete data point for investors tracking hotel values in affluent suburban Atlanta submarkets, though the dossier does not disclose per-key pricing, capitalization rates, or financing terms.

The property debuted in 2018 and includes guest rooms and suites ranging from 355 to 1,100 square feet. Reported amenities include a second-floor swimming pool, a fitness center, an on-site restaurant, event venues, and three and a half levels of parking with 407 spaces. The building also contains the Alpharetta Conference Center, a 65,000-square-foot corporate venue developed in partnership with the city of Alpharetta. That conference component offers 44,000 square feet of flexible meeting space, 18 separate rooms, a ballroom, and capacity for up to 1,200 people, making the asset more than a traditional rooms-driven hotel.

The Hotel at Avalon sits within the Avalon mixed-use district, described in the source as a $1 billion, 86-acre walkable neighborhood comprising roughly 2.3 million square feet of retail, dining, office, and entertainment space, plus more than 600 luxury rental apartments. This context helps explain the asset's appeal: the hotel is embedded in a dense, amenity-rich environment that can support both leisure and corporate demand. The source is Connect CRE, a secondary trade publication, and it attributes the sale reporting to Commercial Search. The dossier contains one full-text source, so the reported $220 million figure and property details are not independently corroborated within the available evidence.

For the broader commercial real estate market, the deal suggests continued investor interest in high-quality hospitality assets located within master-planned, mixed-use environments in the Atlanta region. The presence of a joint venture buyer also indicates that institutional or well-capitalized private investors remain active in suburban hotel acquisitions. However, the evidence does not establish whether the price reflects a premium or discount to prior valuation, nor does it reveal seller motivation, buyer plans, or any capital expenditure requirements. Readers should treat the transaction as a single reported data point rather than a confirmed trend.

Key limitations include the absence of closing documentation, deed records, or buyer and seller statements. The source text contains a minor grammatical omission in describing Crescent's ownership period, and it does not specify whether the $220 million includes the conference center or any separate city interest. Future reporting could clarify the allocation of value between hotel operations and conference facilities, the joint venture's operating strategy, and whether the sale price implies a meaningful gain or loss for Crescent since its September 2020 acquisition.
