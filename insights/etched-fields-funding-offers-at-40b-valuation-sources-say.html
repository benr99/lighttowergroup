Etched fields funding offers at $40B+ valuation, sources say

Just a couple of months after its last big raise, the AI chip startup is already being plied with investment offers at double or more its current value, sources

Ben Rohr October 06, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Just a couple of months after raising $700 million at a $21 billion valuation, AI chip startup Etched is already reviewing incoming investment offers that would value the company at roughly double that amount or more, according to people familiar with the company. The development matters because it signals unusually intense investor demand for a four-year-old startup attempting to challenge Nvidia in AI inference hardware, and because the speed of the re-rating suggests backers are competing to secure exposure before a potential next financing closes.

Etched is reviewing bids that range from $40 billion from top-tier investors to $50 billion from lesser-known backers, one person said. The talks are early, so terms of any deal, should one happen, may change. Etched declined to comment. The company's last two rounds illustrate the pace: a $300 million round at a $10.3 billion valuation led by Sequoia was announced in July, followed by the $700 million round at a $21 billion valuation in September. A person familiar with the offers said that if Etched raises as much as its last round, this could give the company a cushion of as much as 3.5 years of runway.

The evidence for the reported offers comes from a single TechCrunch report citing people familiar with the company, with one person providing the $40 billion to $50 billion bid range. The report does not identify the investors by name, does not specify how many offers have been received, and does not confirm whether Etched has accepted any terms. The underlying business facts are more concrete: Etched said in July that it had secured $1 billion in customer orders, including one from Jane Street, after manufacturing its test chip at a TSMC factory this summer. Jane Street led the last $700 million round and is also a customer that took delivery of an early system.

The reported interest fits a broader pattern of back-to-back funding rounds that essentially act as a single financing split into two tranches with separate valuations, a structure the report notes is increasingly common among the buzziest startups. Etched's appeal rests on claims that its chips can process more tokens faster and at a lower cost than Nvidia's, a proposition attractive to Jane Street, where a microscopic speed advantage can yield massive profits. The company has also drawn roughly 15% of its 400-person workforce from Nvidia, according to The Wall Street Journal, and operates a new 10-megawatt data center in Silicon Valley plus a facility in Taiwan to coordinate production near TSMC.

The main limitation is that the reported offers are unconfirmed by Etched and rest on a single unnamed source. There is no independent corroboration of the $40 billion to $50 billion range, no disclosed term sheet, and no indication of which top-tier or lesser-known investors are involved. What to watch is whether Etched accepts a new round, at what valuation, and whether any disclosed terms confirm or revise the reported range. Until then, the $40 billion-plus figure should be treated as an early, changeable signal of demand rather than an established market price.
