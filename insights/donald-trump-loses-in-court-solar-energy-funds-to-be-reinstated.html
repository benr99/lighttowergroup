Donald Trump Loses in Court, Solar Energy Funds to Be Reinstated

A federal judge in Rhode Island has ruled against the Trump administration's effort to rescind $7 billion in funding for the Solar For All program, according to a CleanTechnica report. The decision matters because the program is designed to expand affordable solar energy access to more than 900,000 households across the country. The ruling, issued by a Trump-appointed judge on the U.S. District Court for the District of Rhode Island, represents a legal setback for the administration's broader push to curtail renewable energy funding, even as domestic solar production has continued to grow.

The Solar For All program provides grant funding to regional entities to deploy solar energy in underserved communities. Last summer, the Environmental Protection Agency under Donald Trump announced it would rescind the funds and ended the program. The court's ruling now blocks that rescission, clearing a path for the $7 billion in grant funding to be reinstated. The decision does not appear to create new funding, but rather restores access to money that had been allocated before the administration moved to terminate the program.

The evidence for this development comes from a single CleanTechnica article, which is a secondary source in the clean technology media space. The report includes a statement from Sierra Club Climate Policy Director Patrick Drupp, who said: "Solar energy is cheap, reliable, and quick to get online. It is a common sense solution to address the record-high electricity bills that families are seeing, while improving public health and cleaning up our air and water." Drupp added that the Sierra Club was glad the courts agreed that the administration's attempt to end Solar For All was unlawful. The article also notes that in June 2026, U.S. solar production overtook coal production for the first month on record, a data point that underscores the sector's momentum despite federal policy headwinds.

For the solar energy sector, the ruling could reduce regulatory uncertainty for grant recipients and project developers that had been left in limbo after the EPA's rescission. The reinstatement of $7 billion in funding may support continued deployment of residential and community solar projects, particularly in regions that rely on Solar For All grants to make installations affordable. The decision also signals that courts may serve as a check on executive branch efforts to unwind clean energy programs, which could have implications for other renewable energy initiatives facing similar administrative challenges.

The analysis is limited by the thin evidentiary base: the dossier contains only one source read in full, and key details such as the judge's name, the specific legal reasoning, the timeline for fund disbursement, and whether the administration will appeal are not provided. The article does not include the full court order or independent confirmation from the EPA or the Department of Justice. Readers should watch for whether the administration appeals the ruling, how quickly the EPA moves to restore the grant funding, and whether other solar or clean energy programs facing similar rescission attempts are affected by this precedent.
