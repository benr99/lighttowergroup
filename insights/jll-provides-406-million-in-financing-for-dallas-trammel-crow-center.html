JLL provides $406 million in financing for Dallas’ Trammel Crow Center

JLL’s Capital Markets group has arranged $406 million in financing for Trammell Crow Center, a 1,226,825-square-foot trophy office tower in Dallas’ Arts Distric

Ben Rohr September 29, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

JLL’s Capital Markets group has arranged $406 million in financing for Trammell Crow Center, a 1,226,825-square-foot trophy office tower in Dallas’ highly sought-after Arts District. The financing matters because it shows continued lender appetite for well-leased, Class AA office assets in a market where office debt has faced broader scrutiny. The deal was arranged on behalf of the borrower, Regent Properties, LLC, and took the form of a five-year, fixed-rate, single-asset single-borrower loan. The property is reported to be 92% leased to a diverse roster of creditworthy tenants, including Goldman Sachs, Baker & Botts LLP, Vinson & Elkins LLP, Orix and Prudential.

The mechanics of the transaction are specific but limited in the available source. JLL secured the five-year, fixed-rate SASB loan through Wells Fargo and Morgan Stanley. The asset is a 50-story tower occupying a 2.2-acre site at the confluence of Dallas’ most important districts. The property continues to benefit from a transformative $182 million renovation completed in 2019 by the prior ownership group. That renovation included a new parking structure providing a 3.0 per 1,000 square feet parking ratio, 27,000 square feet of ground-floor retail, a complete lobby renovation, and a full suite of Class AA amenities such as the TCC Athletic Club, conference center, outdoor pavilion and on-site dining options.

The evidence comes from a single secondary source, REJournals, which published the announcement on September 29, 2026. The source identifies the JLL Capital Markets Debt Advisory team as Managing Director Greg Napper and Associates Jordan Buck and Rett Daugbjerg. The dossier does not include loan pricing, maturity date, debt yield, loan-to-value, or any borrower or lender statements. The $406 million financing figure and the $182 million renovation figure are both drawn directly from the source, but neither is corroborated by a second independent outlet in the available evidence.

The market implication is that a trophy Arts District office tower can still attract large single-asset financing when occupancy and tenant credit are strong. Trammell Crow Center’s location is described as the epicenter of Dallas’ Arts District, overlooking Klyde Warren Park and the Nasher Sculpture Center. The source says the property has seamless connectivity to over 400 shops and restaurants, 2.7 million square feet of retail and more than 29,000 multifamily units within walking distance. The 118-acre Dallas Arts District is described as the largest contiguous urban arts district in the United States, attracting over 1.5 million visitors annually and generating an estimated $400 million in economic impact. Those figures are presented by the source as context for the asset’s appeal, not as independently verified transaction metrics.

What remains unknown is significant. The source does not state whether the financing is a refinancing or an acquisition loan, what the proceeds will be used for, or how the loan was structured beyond the five-year fixed-rate SASB label. It also does not provide the property’s valuation, net operating income, or debt service coverage. Because the evidence level is single_full_text, the analysis must stay close to one source and cannot confirm whether the $406 million loan represents new debt or a replacement of existing financing. The key items to watch are whether additional sources disclose loan terms, whether the borrower or lenders confirm the transaction, and whether the Dallas office market continues to support large SASB executions for well-leased trophy assets.
