Walker & Dunlop Closes $631M Portfolio Refi for IMT Capital

Ben Rohr October 05, 2026 2 min read 1 source

Walker & Dunlop, Inc. said Monday it arranged $630,618,000 in fixed-rate loans from Fannie Mae to refinance nine multifamily properties across six states on behalf of IMT Capital. The portfolio totals 3,528 units in Florida, Arizona, Texas, Colorado, California and Tennessee. The announcement matters because it shows continued execution of large, geographically diversified multifamily refinancings through agency lending, even as individual transactions were coordinated across a four-month closing window rather than as a single simultaneous deal.

The financing was structured as nine separate transactions, each a five-year, fixed-rate loan with full-term interest-only payments and a 35-year amortization. The loans closed between May 1 and Sept. 1, 2026. A Walker & Dunlop Capital Markets Real Estate Finance team led by Cory Wizenberg, Matt Wallach, Stephen West, Walker Layne, AJ Wright and Sebastian Tamayo worked with Fannie Mae to execute the portfolio financings. The consistent structure across all nine loans was intended to provide IMT Capital with long-term flexibility across the portfolio while coordinating the transactions over the four-month period.

The evidence comes from a single secondary source, Connect CRE, which reported the announcement based on Walker & Dunlop's statement. The source quotes Matt Wallach: "Working alongside IMT Capital and Fannie Mae, we were able to execute multiple financings while providing a structure tailored to IMT's broader portfolio strategy." The report also notes that the transaction followed another portfolio refinancing of comparable scale executed by Walker & Dunlop on behalf of IMT in the second half of 2025. The article includes a representative property reference, IMT Desert Ridge in Phoenix, which IMT Capital acquired in January 2025, though the dossier does not confirm whether that specific property was among the nine refinanced assets.

For the multifamily lending market, the deal signals continued appetite from Fannie Mae for large portfolio refinancings with standardized terms. The use of full-term interest-only payments and 35-year amortization across nine assets in six states suggests agency lenders remain willing to accommodate sponsor portfolio strategies that prioritize cash flow flexibility over near-term principal paydown. The geographic spread across Florida, Arizona, Texas, Colorado, California and Tennessee also indicates that the underlying collateral spans both high-growth Sun Belt markets and more established coastal and mountain region assets, though the dossier does not provide property-level detail or loan-to-value metrics.

The analysis is limited by the thin evidentiary base. The dossier contains no information on interest rates, debt yield, loan-to-value ratios, property performance, or IMT Capital's broader portfolio strategy beyond the quoted statement. The source is a secondary trade publication, and the underlying announcement from Walker & Dunlop was not independently reviewed. The exact allocation of the $630,618,000 across the nine properties is not disclosed, nor is the identity of all nine assets. What to watch is whether Walker & Dunlop or IMT Capital provides additional detail on property-level performance, whether the 2025 comparable refinancing establishes a recurring agency lending relationship, and how the full-term interest-only structure performs if multifamily rent growth or occupancy softens in any of the six states.
