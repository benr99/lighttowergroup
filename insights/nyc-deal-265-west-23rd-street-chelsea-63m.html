A&E Real Estate (Douglas Eisenberg) Takes Down Chelsea's 265 West 23rd Street for $63.5M at $645 a Foot

Backed by a $41.27 million loan from Fannie Mae, the Chelsea trade clears at $645 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

Along West 23rd Street between Seventh and Eighth Avenues, free-market elevator apartment buildings rarely change hands unless a giant sponsor is tidying up an older fund vintage. Douglas Eisenberg's A&E; Real Estate stepped in to buy the 118-unit Chelsea building from Related Companies for $63.5 million ($538,136 per unit), capturing a 10.6 percent discount to its 2015 valuation. When the deed closed on March 2, 2026, A&E; Real Estate (Douglas Eisenberg) had officially taken down 265 West 23rd Street from Related Companies for $63.5 million, with Eastdil Secured brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 98,500-square-foot building last traded in August 2015 for $71 million. Stepping in at $645 a square foot—or $538,136 a door across its 114 apartments— shaves 10.6 percent off that prior valuation, giving A&E; Real Estate (Douglas Eisenberg) a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle. Even better for the buyers, the in-place rent roll hands them a verified 5.60 percent going-in cap rate from day one.

Combining a CEMA Assignment With Wells Fargo Agency Debt

How you structure the debt on a Manhattan acquisition matters almost as much as what you pay for the deed. Fannie Mae (via Wells Fargo Multifamily Capital) stepped up with a $41.27 million senior loan—representing 65 percent loan-to-value—while the buyers wrote a $22.23 million equity check behind it.

Rather than extinguishing the seller's old note and originating a scratch mortgage, the buyers and Fannie Mae kept the existing mortgage wrapper alive through a Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement, or CEMA. Because New York City levies a roughly 2.8 percent mortgage recording tax on new commercial debt, that single piece of legal plumbing saved the deal upwards of $1.16 million at the closing table—cash that stays in the property's capex and leasing reserve instead of going to the Department of Finance.

From Our Desk

When we look at 265 West 23rd Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and lender selection. A&E; Real Estate (Douglas Eisenberg) didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Chelsea footage at $645 a square foot and paired it with the exact lender bucket built for this business plan. Pairing a Fannie Mae execution with a CEMA assignment on a 23rd Street corridor building is textbook New York defensive offense: 5.6 percent day-one yield with zero mortgage recording tax leakage.
