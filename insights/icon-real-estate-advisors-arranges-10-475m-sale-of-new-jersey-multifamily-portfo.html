Icon Real Estate Advisors Arranges $10.475M Sale of New Jersey Multifamily Portfolio

A lender-controlled REO sale of two East Orange multifamily properties transfers 88 units to MNM Holding LLC, with roughly 51% vacancy setting up a renovation a

Ben Rohr October 11, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

ICON Real Estate Advisors has arranged the $10.475 million sale of an 88-unit multifamily portfolio at 255 and 284 Prospect Street in East Orange, New Jersey. The transaction matters because it is a foreclosure-driven REO sale: MNM Holding LLC acquired the properties from a lender-controlled entity, meaning the assets moved through a distressed ownership transition rather than a conventional arm's-length trade between operating landlords. That context helps explain the portfolio's reported vacancy of approximately 51% at the time of sale, a condition that would typically deter stabilized-asset buyers but creates a value-add opportunity for an investor prepared to renovate and re-lease a substantial portion of the units.

The mechanics of the deal are unusually relationship-driven. ICON Managing Partner David Oropeza exclusively represented the seller, while ICON Director, Multifamily David Betesh represented the buyer. The sale marks the fifth time Oropeza has sold the Prospect Street properties over his career and Betesh's third completed transaction with the buyer. The two buildings sit along Prospect Street, described as one of East Orange's established residential corridors, and contain a mix of studio, one-bedroom, and two-bedroom residences. The buyer's stated business plan is to undertake a substantial renovation and lease-up program across the properties, leveraging the scale of 88 units along a single corridor.

The evidence for this transaction comes from a single secondary source, Yield PRO, published on October 10, 2026. The article is a full-text trade-publication report rather than a primary filing or broker offering memorandum, so details such as the foreclosure timeline, the lender's identity, the prior ownership structure, and the exact unit mix are not disclosed. The dossier does not include a per-unit price breakdown, but the headline figure implies roughly $119,000 per unit based on the stated 88 units and $10.475 million sale price. The source also reports that the transaction brings ICON's East Orange multifamily sales volume to more than $50 million year to date, encompassing approximately 12 properties across six separate transactions, which provides a limited but useful benchmark for the firm's activity in that submarket.

For the broader multifamily sector, the deal illustrates how distressed REO inventory is being absorbed by private buyers with renovation capacity. The 51% vacancy figure is the most material market signal in the dossier: it suggests either prior operational distress or deliberate de-tenanting ahead of a repositioning, though the source does not specify which. The buyer's willingness to acquire a half-vacant portfolio from a lender-controlled seller indicates that capital remains available for workforce-housing value-add plays in northern New Jersey, even when occupancy is weak. ICON's framing of the deal as part of "momentum" across its business, with assignments spanning individual assets and larger multifamily portfolios, points to continued transaction flow in the region, but the article offers no independent market data on pricing trends, cap rates, or rent growth.

Several limitations should be noted. The dossier contains no corroborating source beyond the single Yield PRO article, and the article itself appears to rely substantially on statements from the brokers involved. Quotes from Oropeza and Betesh are promotional in nature and should be read as deal-team commentary rather than independent analysis. Key unknowns include the foreclosure date, the lender-controlled entity's identity, the prior loan amount, the renovation budget, projected post-renovation rents, and the buyer's financing structure. The $20 billion in combined career transaction volume attributed to ICON's partners is a firm-level credential, not a transaction-specific data point. What to watch next is whether the buyer executes the lease-up program and whether subsequent East Orange REO sales price at similar or improving per-unit levels, which would indicate whether this $10.475 million trade reflects a one-off distressed discount or a broader clearing level for vacant multifamily assets in the submarket.
