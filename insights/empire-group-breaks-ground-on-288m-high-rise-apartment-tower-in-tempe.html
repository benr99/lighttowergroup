Empire Group Breaks Ground on $288M High-Rise Apartment Tower in Tempe

Aspirant Development, the luxury infill division of Empire Group, has started construction on Revelry, a two-tower, 533-unit Class A high-rise community in Temp

Ben Rohr October 11, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

One of the Empire group of companies has broken ground on a two-tower, $288 million luxury apartment project in Tempe, Arizona, marking a significant capital commitment to high-rise rental housing in a market anchored by Arizona State University. Aspirant Development, the luxury infill division of Empire Group, is building the 533-unit Class A community called Revelry. The project matters because it signals continued developer appetite for dense, amenity-heavy multifamily product in Tempe, even as high-rise construction carries elevated cost and execution risk relative to garden-style or mid-rise alternatives.

The development consists of a 15-story tower and a 17-story tower on a single site. Financing for the project amounted to $229 million, according to the source, leaving a substantial equity or other capital component relative to the stated $288 million project value. Gensler is the designer and Layton is the general contractor. The two towers will be connected by a 27,000-square-foot pool deck on the second floor. Planned amenities include a clubhouse with a spa and fitness center, a two-lane bowling alley, pickleball courts, wellness spaces, sauna, cold plunge, aromatherapy rooms, a yoga terrace, lounges with a bar and fireplace, a golf simulator, an arcade, a dog spa, co-working spaces, and a coffee bar. The project also includes approximately 5,500 square feet of first-floor retail space across two suites and an additional 10,800 square feet on the second floor.

The evidence for this development comes from a single secondary trade source, Yield PRO, which published the announcement on October 10, 2026. The dossier identifies the source as tier 4 and secondary, meaning the information has not been independently corroborated by primary documents such as municipal permits, lender statements, or developer filings. The only direct quote in the record comes from Geoffrey Jacobs, Managing Partner of Aspirant Development, who said the project reflects a belief that successful urban high-rise projects must be intentional in design, amenities, and neighborhood engagement. He cited the location at one of Tempe's most active intersections, direct access to ASU, and a dense surrounding population as factors supporting the project. No additional quotes, financing terms, or third-party market data are available in the source.

For the Tempe multifamily market, the groundbreaking suggests that developers still see room for Class A urban rental supply near a major university and employment center. The project is explicitly designed to rival top urban high-rise communities nationwide, according to the source, which implies a competitive positioning strategy aimed at renters willing to pay a premium for location and lifestyle amenities. However, the article does not provide rent projections, absorption assumptions, construction timelines, or capitalization rates, so the financial underwriting cannot be assessed from the available evidence. The gap between the $288 million project value and the $229 million financing figure also raises an open question about the capital stack, but the source does not explain that difference.

The main limitation of this analysis is the thin evidentiary base. Because only one secondary source was read in full, details such as unit mix, parking, sustainability features, delivery dates, and lender identity remain unknown. Future reporting should watch for confirmation from Tempe planning records, construction progress updates, and any disclosure of lease-up performance once the first tower delivers. Until then, the Revelry groundbreaking should be read as a directional signal of developer confidence in Tempe's urban rental market rather than as a fully verified financial transaction.
