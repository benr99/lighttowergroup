Fulton Mall Blockbuster: Four Eighty Five LLC Drops $83.5M on 147 Lawrence Street Assemblage

With BCC 485 Fulton LLC supplying $26.35 million in acquisition financing, Four Eighty Five LLC locks in 147 Lawrence Street at $746 a square foot.

At the intersection of Lawrence and Fulton streets, where the Downtown Brooklyn rezoning sent glass residential towers climbing 60 stories overhead, low-rise commercial parcels with C6-4.5 zoning are the last remaining puzzle pieces on the Fulton Mall. Four Eighty Five LLC closed on the two-parcel commercial and educational assemblage at 147 Lawrence Street (paired with 485 Fulton Street) on September 16, 2026 for $83.5 million, taking title from Fulton Street Realty Venture, L.L.C. at $746 per existing square foot.

Run the numbers across 112,000 square feet and the buyer's thesis comes into view. At $746 a square foot on a 25,400-square-foot parcel zoned C6-4.5, Four Eighty Five LLC is locking in irreplaceable Downtown Brooklyn brick well below new-construction replacement cost.

A $57.15 Million Equity Check Backed by Bain Capital Credit

To close the $83.5 million purchase, the buyers tapped BCC 485 Fulton LLC—a special-purpose lending affiliate backed by Bain Capital Credit—for a $26.35 million senior acquisition loan, sizing senior debt to a conservative 31.6 percent loan-to-value.

Putting $57.15 million of hard equity behind a 31.6 percent private-credit note is a classic Downtown Brooklyn pre-development play. Under C6-4.5 zoning—one of the highest commercial and residential floor-area-ratio designations in Brooklyn—a 25,400-square-foot footprint on Fulton Mall can support more than 300,000 square feet of vertical tower density steps from four subway lines.

From Our Desk

Looking at 147 Lawrence Street from our debt and equity desk at Light Tower Group, one takeaway stands out for sponsors sizing up Downtown Brooklyn. When a Downtown Brooklyn C6-4.5 assemblage clears for $83.5 million with 68 percent hard equity and a bespoke $26.35 million private credit facility, the buyer isn't just clipping retail coupons—they are banking one of the highest-FAR corners left on Fulton Street.
