Four Eighty Five LLC Closes $83.5 million Purchase of 147 Lawrence Street in Downtown Brooklyn

Backed by a $26.35 million loan from BCC 485 Fulton LLC, the Downtown Brooklyn trade clears at $746 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

At the intersection of Lawrence and Fulton streets, where the Downtown Brooklyn rezoning sent glass residential towers climbing 60 stories overhead, low-rise commercial parcels with C6-4.5 zoning are the last remaining puzzle pieces on the Fulton Mall. Four Eighty Five LLC closed on the two-parcel commercial and educational assemblage at 147 Lawrence Street (paired with 485 Fulton Street) on September 16, 2026 for $83.5 million, taking title from Fulton Street Realty Venture, L.L.C. at $746 per existing square foot. When the deed closed on September 16, 2026, Four Eighty Five LLC had officially taken down 147 Lawrence Street from Fulton Street Realty Venture, L.L.C. for $83.5 million, with Institutional Capital Markets brokering the trade.

At $746 a square foot across 112,000 square feet, the trade establishes a clean clearing mark for Downtown Brooklyn fulton mall commercial & educational assemblage assets.

A $57.15 Million Equity Check Backed by Private Credit

The real tell in any Brooklyn closing sits on the debt side of the ledger. To fund the purchase, Four Eighty Five LLC locked in a $26.35 million senior acquisition credit facility from BCC 485 Fulton LLC (Bain Capital Credit Affiliate) at 31.6 percent loan-to-value.

That loan commitment meant the buyers only needed to write a $57.15 million equity check to control a 4-story, 1988-vintage Downtown Brooklyn asset. Credit committees aren't stretching to 75 percent leverage anymore, but when a sponsor brings $57.15 million of real cash equity and a basis of $746 a square foot, lenders like BCC 485 Fulton LLC are wide open for business.

From Our Desk

When we look at 147 Lawrence Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and capital structure. Four Eighty Five LLC didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Downtown Brooklyn footage at $746 a square foot and structured the stack to match the asset's timeline. When a Downtown Brooklyn C6-4.5 assemblage clears for $83.5 million with 68 percent hard equity and a bespoke $26.35 million private credit facility, the buyer isn't just clipping retail coupons—they are banking one of the highest-FAR corners left on Fulton Street.
