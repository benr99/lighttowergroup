Dwight Investment Management Provides $42M Refi for College Station Apartments

Partin Development has closed $42 million of bridge debt from Dwight Investment Management to refinance the 288-unit 1720 at Harvey property near Texas A&M Univ

Ben Rohr September 23, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Partin Development has sealed $42 million of bridge debt to refinance a newly built multifamily asset near Texas A&M; University, according to Commercial Observer. The loan, supplied by Dwight Investment Management, covers the 288-unit 1720 at Harvey property in College Station, Texas, which opened in 2023. The transaction matters because it shows how sponsors of recently delivered student-oriented housing are using short-term bridge capital to stabilize assets and extract cash before transitioning to agency or government-backed permanent financing.

The debt was originated by Dwight's Katie Goldenberg and will refinance existing debt while also providing cash-out proceeds to the borrower, according to Dwight. Greystone Capital Advisors' Eric Rosenstock negotiated the financing. "A bridge was the proper play for this asset as we prepare for a long-term HUD loan," Rosenstock said in a statement. "Partin is a long-term focused real estate group that understands multifamily longevity." The property is located at 1720 Summit Crossing Lane, about a mile from the Texas A&M; campus, with apartments ranging from one to three bedrooms. Community amenities include swimming pools, a fitness center, a yoga studio, a clubhouse, a business center, a pet park, and grilling areas.

The Commercial Observer report is the sole source for this transaction, and it provides limited financial detail beyond the loan amount and the borrower's stated path toward a HUD loan. The deal closed nearly two years after Partin secured $24 million of acquisition financing from Benefit Street Partners for the project, according to the outlet's earlier reporting. That prior financing figure offers a useful benchmark: the new $42 million bridge loan is substantially larger than the acquisition debt, suggesting either increased project value, higher leverage, or a combination of refinancing and cash-out proceeds. However, the report does not disclose the property's current valuation, loan-to-value ratio, interest rate, term, or the exact amount of cash-out proceeds.

For the broader multifamily and student housing sector, the transaction highlights continued lender appetite for well-located assets near large public universities, even as many markets face elevated capital costs. Texas A&M;'s enrollment scale and College Station's rental demand provide a stable demand base that can support bridge lending. The stated plan to move toward a long-term HUD loan also reflects a common capital stack strategy: use flexible bridge debt to manage near-term cash flows and seasoning requirements, then refinance into lower-cost, longer-duration government-insured financing once the asset demonstrates stabilized operations.

Several limitations temper the analysis. The dossier contains only one full-text source, and key terms such as loan maturity, interest rate, amortization, and prepayment structure are not disclosed. Partin Development did not immediately return requests for comment, leaving the borrower's own perspective unrepresented beyond the broker's statement. The report also does not specify whether the cash-out proceeds will be used for property improvements, distributions, or other corporate purposes. Future reporting should clarify the HUD loan timeline, the property's current occupancy and rent levels, and whether the bridge loan includes extension options or other structural features that could affect refinancing risk.
