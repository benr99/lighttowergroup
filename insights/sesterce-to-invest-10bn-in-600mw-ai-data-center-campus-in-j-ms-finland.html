Sesterce to invest $10bn in 600MW AI data center campus in Jämsä, Finland

French AI infrastructure company Sesterce has announced a $10 billion AI data center campus in Jämsä, Finland, with an anchor customer already secured, accordin

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Sesterce, a French AI and supercomputing infrastructure company, has announced plans for a $10 billion AI data center campus in Jämsä, Finland. The project would be built on the site of the former Kaipola paper mill, with work expected to begin this year. The announcement matters because it signals continued appetite for large-scale AI compute capacity in the Nordics, where power availability, cool climate, and existing industrial infrastructure are attractive to data center developers. However, the evidence base is limited to a single secondary source, so the announcement should be read as a development plan rather than a fully financed, operational commitment.

The material facts are specific but not independently corroborated. Sesterce said the campus will have a capacity of 200MW during its first phase, with an expected increase to 600MW in phase two. The company also said it aims to build more than 1GW of capacity in Finland long term. Alongside the capacity figures, Sesterce committed to enabling one megawatt of newly built renewable generation capacity on the Finnish power grid for every megawatt the campus consumes within 10 years of becoming operational. The site is planned to feature a closed-loop liquid cooling system supplemented with harvested rainwater and recycled water. Sesterce will also establish a $10 million community fund to finance local projects in Jämsä.

The source context is important. The only available evidence is a full-text article from Data Center Dynamics, a secondary trade publication. The article reports that Sesterce said it has already secured an anchor customer for the site, but the customer is not named and no contract terms are disclosed. The report also quotes Sesterce CEO Youssef El Manssouri, who framed the project as preserving the area's industrial identity while using existing infrastructure, including the power grid, water systems, buildings, and logistics connections. Jämsä Mayor Jori Reijula offered a cautious but supportive statement, saying the project is an interesting opportunity provided it progresses from plans to implementation and meets applicable requirements.

For the data center sector, the announcement reinforces a pattern of large AI campus proposals in Finland and the broader Nordic region. The planned 600MW campus would be substantial relative to many existing facilities, and the renewable energy matching commitment is notable because power availability and sustainability are central constraints for AI infrastructure. The reference to a prior $471 million investment by Sesterce in an AI data center in Valence, France, suggests the company has some track record, but the scale difference between that project and the $10 billion Jämsä plan is significant. The anchor customer claim, if accurate, would reduce demand risk, but without a named counterparty or contract details, it remains an unverified assertion.

The main limitation is that the entire analysis rests on one secondary source. There is no confirmation from Sesterce's own filings, no Finnish regulatory documentation, and no independent reporting on financing, power purchase agreements, or construction timelines. The mayor's statement explicitly conditions support on the project progressing from plans to implementation and meeting requirements, which indicates local approval is not yet complete. What to watch includes whether Sesterce names the anchor customer, whether phase one construction actually begins this year, and whether the renewable energy matching commitment is formalized in binding agreements. Until those details emerge, the $10 billion figure should be treated as a planned investment, not a completed capital deployment.
