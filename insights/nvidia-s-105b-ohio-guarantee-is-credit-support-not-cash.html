Nvidia’s $105B Ohio Guarantee Is Credit Support, Not Cash

The residual-value backstop aims to unlock financing for OpenAI’s initial 4.25 GW Ohio campus.

Ben Rohr August 20, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

One chart, one argument

The $105 billion that Nvidia disclosed for OpenAI’s Ohio data center campus sounds like a cash investment. It is not. It is a residual-value guarantee: a conditional backstop tied to the leased infrastructure, not upfront construction funding. That distinction changes how the deal should be read.

The guarantee covers an initial 4.25 GW of IT capacity at SB Energy’s PORTS-Pike Technology Campus in Pike County, Ohio. Nvidia has an option to support another 3.75 GW, which would bring the campus to roughly 8 GW. SB Energy will build, own and operate the site under a 20-year lease with OpenAI. Nvidia said it has secured land, power and shell capacity at the site and will be the exclusive AI compute infrastructure provider.

The aggregate payment obligation for the initial guarantees is capped at $105 billion. The payment is conditional. If specified default or insolvency events occur, Nvidia may owe the shortfall between the guaranteed minimum value under the leases and what SB Energy recovers by re-leasing or selling the facilities. OpenAI has agreed to reimburse and indemnify Nvidia for amounts Nvidia actually pays. In other words, Nvidia is providing credit support for the lease, not writing a $105 billion check.

The structure is separate from Nvidia’s cash outlay. Nvidia is investing $1.5 billion in SB Energy. The residual-value guarantee is a distinct contractual commitment. It is designed to support financing for long-lived infrastructure rather than pay for construction directly. The actual guarantee agreements have not been filed yet; Nvidia said they will be included in its quarterly report for the period ended July 26.

Nvidia’s stated logic is that the infrastructure will outlive any single generation of chips. CEO Jensen Huang said the company is “securing long-lived infrastructure for Nvidia compute so OpenAI can deploy the most productive AI factories that can be upgraded repeatedly.” Steven Dickens, CEO of HyperFrame Research, told Data Center Knowledge that GPUs retain value after initial use, similar to Ford Finance’s approach to vehicles, and that AI hardware useful lives can run six to seven years or longer. That residual value creates room for financing models that do not treat the hardware as worthless after the first deployment.

The most important open question is not whether Nvidia can afford the $105 billion cap. It is how much financing capacity the guarantee actually creates. The answer will depend on the guaranteed minimum values and trigger definitions in the un-filed agreements. On the power side, SB Energy and SoftBank have separately said they plan to develop at least 10 GW of new energy generation to support the project. But the SB Energy and SoftBank disclosure does not include an interconnection timeline or a firm delivery date, which is where the project’s schedule risk may sit.

The quarterly exhibit will be the test. If the guaranteed minimum values are high and the trigger events are narrow, the structure could meaningfully reduce lease financing cost. If the recovery assumptions are optimistic, the $105 billion figure may overstate the real credit support.
