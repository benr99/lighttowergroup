Standing shoulder to shoulder with 782 Lexington Avenue just steps from the 59th Street–Lexington Avenue subway express hub, 780 Lexington Avenue provided the southern anchor for a $23.73 million two-building assemblage. Closed concurrently on August 27, 2026, 780 Lex Realty Associates LLC paid $10.09 million ($2,424 per square foot) to take title to the four-story mixed-use building at 780 Lexington Avenue from 780 Lex Partnership L.P., which also provided the $8.9 million seller-financed mortgage covering the assemblage.

Every Manhattan submarket has a number where institutional buyers stop watching from the sidelines and start wiring earnest money. In Lenox Hill / Plaza District, that clearing level is $2,424 a square foot (or $3,364,729 per apartment across 2 residential units) across 4,165 square feet under C5-2 zoning.

Why Seller Financing Is Unlocking $2,400-a-Foot Upper East Side Assemblages

On a standalone basis, the $8.9 million mortgage recorded under ACRIS document 2026090100549004 represents 88.2 percent of 780 Lexington's $10.09 million price—or 37.5 percent across the combined $23.73 million two-building purchase with 782 Lexington Avenue.

Seller-financed paper has re-emerged in 2026 as one of the smartest ways to bridge bid-ask spreads on boutique Manhattan retail buildings. Legacy family partnerships avoid an immediate capital-gains hit while collecting senior interest secured by the real estate they know best.

From Our Desk

For sponsors watching the Manhattan tape from our desk at Light Tower Group, 780 Lex Realty Associates LLC's move at 780 Lexington Avenue sends a clear signal. When a long-term partnership rolls its equity into an $8.9 million purchase-money note to help an assembler unite 780 and 782 Lexington Avenue, both sides win: the seller collects fixed-rate interest on a prime Lenox Hill corner, and the buyer controls contiguous C5-2 frontage two blocks from Bloomingdale's.
