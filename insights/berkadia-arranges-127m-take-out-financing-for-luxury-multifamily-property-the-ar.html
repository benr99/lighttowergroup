Berkadia Arranges $127M Take-Out Financing for Luxury Multifamily Property The Arcadian in Fort Lauderdale

Berkadia arranged $127 million in construction take-out financing from Madison Realty Capital for The Arcadian, a newly built 502-unit mixed-use multifamily com

Ben Rohr October 05, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Berkadia announced that it arranged $127 million in construction take-out financing from Madison Realty Capital for The Arcadian, a newly built 502-unit mixed-use multifamily community in Fort Lauderdale's Sistrunk District. The loan refinances the property's existing construction debt and provides capital as The Arcadian continues lease-up and moves toward stabilization. The transaction matters because it shows how private credit lenders are stepping into the take-out financing role for newly completed multifamily assets at a moment when lease-up timelines and stabilization paths are under close scrutiny. For sponsors Fuse Group and KREA Developments, the financing converts construction risk into an operating-phase capital structure, giving the project runway to reach stabilized occupancy without an immediate sale or recapitalization.

The financing was arranged by Managing Directors Scott Wadler and Brad Williamson, Vice President Bobby Dockerty and Analyst Nicholas Horowitz of Berkadia Miami. The two-building community received temporary certificate of occupancy in early 2026. Located at 640 NW 7th Avenue, The Arcadian comprises 502 studio, one- and two-bedroom apartments across two eight-story buildings, including 151 apartments designated as attainable housing for qualified households earning between 100% and 120% of area median income. The development also includes approximately 15,000 square feet of ground-floor commercial space intended for neighborhood-serving businesses and entrepreneurs. Amenities include a resort-style pool deck, outdoor LED video wall, 24/7 fitness center, indoor pickleball court, dual golf simulators, sauna, private clubhouse, coworking spaces, and a meditation courtyard.

The evidence for this transaction comes from a single full-text source, Yield PRO, published on October 5, 2026. The source quotes Berkadia's Scott Wadler describing The Arcadian as "a significant project not only because of its scale, but because of what Fuse Group and KREA have created in a part of Fort Lauderdale that is undergoing meaningful investment and transformation." Madison Realty Capital's Samir Tejpaul, Head of Investments, said the firm is "built to provide capital at every stage of a project's life cycle, from land acquisition and construction through lease-up and stabilization." The source also notes that The Arcadian received a $10 million investment from the Fort Lauderdale Community Redevelopment Agency and incorporates a pedestrian-oriented Paseo connecting its residential and commercial components. Madison Realty Capital manages $24 billion in assets as of June 30, 2026, and has completed $91 billion of real estate transactions since 2004.

The transaction carries implications for the South Florida multifamily market and for the broader construction take-out financing landscape. The Arcadian sits near Flagler Village and Downtown Fort Lauderdale, with access to Las Olas Boulevard, Brightline's Fort Lauderdale station, I-95, and Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport. The presence of 151 attainable housing units within a luxury development reflects a financing and development model that blends market-rate amenities with income-restricted units, supported in part by public investment from the community redevelopment agency. For lenders, the deal illustrates appetite for newly delivered, institutional-quality multifamily assets in supply-constrained urban submarkets, even before full stabilization. Berkadia's role as arranger, rather than balance-sheet lender, also highlights the continued importance of mortgage banking intermediaries in matching sponsors with private credit capital.

Several limitations frame this analysis. The dossier contains only one source, so the financing terms beyond the headline amount—such as interest rate, term, loan-to-cost, or recourse structure—are not disclosed. The source does not provide current occupancy, lease-up pace, or projected stabilization date for The Arcadian. It also does not state the size of the existing construction debt being refinanced or whether any equity was returned to sponsors. The $10 million Fort Lauderdale Community Redevelopment Agency investment is mentioned without detail on its structure or timing. What to watch next includes whether The Arcadian reaches stabilization on schedule, whether the ground-floor commercial space leases as intended, and whether similar take-out financings emerge for other recently completed multifamily projects in Fort Lauderdale's Sistrunk District and nearby submarkets.
