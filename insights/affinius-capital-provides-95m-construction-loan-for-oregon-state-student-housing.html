Affinius Capital Provides $95M Construction Loan for Oregon State Student Housing

Fields Holdings has secured $94.8 million in construction financing to build Zev Corvallis, a 13-story, 262-unit student housing complex on the campus of Oregon

Ben Rohr October 06, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Fields Holdings has secured $94.8 million in construction financing to build Zev Corvallis, a 13-story, 262-unit student housing complex on the campus of Oregon State University in Corvallis, Ore., Commercial Observer can first report. Affinius Capital provided the debt, and no broker was listed on the transaction. The deal matters because it signals continued lender appetite for purpose-built student housing in university markets where enrollment growth and constrained supply support new development, even as broader commercial real estate construction lending remains selective.

The project is located at 516 South West Fourth Street in Corvallis, a tree-lined neighborhood in western Oregon's Willamette Valley that is home to 61,000 residents and defined by the local college and a longtime Hewlett-Packard corporate facility. Zev Corvallis is expected to deliver 667 student beds and will include 4,500 square feet of retail. The asset is scheduled to be completed in fall 2028. The reported financing figure appears as both $95 million and $94.8 million in the source, reflecting rounding in the headline versus the more precise transaction amount.

Tyler Figley, Affinius Capital senior vice president, said in a statement that his firm invested in the project due to growing enrollment at Oregon State University and a lack of new student housing, particularly any high-rise residential developments in the area. "Fields Holdings has assembled a project that will raise the bar for student housing in Corvallis, and we're proud to back its development," Figley added. The source is a single full-text article from Commercial Observer, a tier-one secondary trade publication, published October 5, 2026, and written by Brian Pascus. No additional documents, filings, or independent confirmations are included in the dossier.

The transaction highlights a broader dynamic in student housing finance: lenders are underwriting projects tied to enrollment fundamentals rather than general multifamily demand. Oregon State University's growing enrollment and the absence of new high-rise residential supply in Corvallis are the two stated drivers of Affinius Capital's investment. The inclusion of ground-floor retail also suggests an effort to integrate the development into the surrounding neighborhood rather than create an isolated campus asset. However, the dossier does not provide loan terms, interest rates, loan-to-cost ratios, sponsorship equity, or construction timelines beyond the fall 2028 completion target.

Several limitations apply to this analysis. The evidence level is single full text, meaning all claims rest on one source read in full. The dossier does not include enrollment figures, vacancy rates, rental comparables, or historical construction data for Corvallis. It also does not identify Fields Holdings' track record, the project's total capitalization, or whether the financing is floating or fixed rate. What to watch includes whether the project meets its fall 2028 delivery date, whether the 667 beds lease at projected rents, and whether additional high-rise student housing proposals emerge in Corvallis in response to this development.
