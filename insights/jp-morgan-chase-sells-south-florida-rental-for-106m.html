JP Morgan Chase Sells South Florida Rental for $106M

Ben Rohr September 23, 2026 2 min read 1 source

J.P. Morgan Chase sold a garden-style multifamily property in Wellington, Fla., for $105.5 million after owning it for nearly a quarter century, according to property records. TA Realty purchased the 366-unit Polo Lakes community at 1950 Polo Lake Boulevard, just north of the Mall at Wellington Green and adjacent to Route 441, in a town best known as a leading equestrian hub. The transaction matters because it shows a long-term institutional owner monetizing a South Florida rental asset at a substantial gain while a Boston-based buyer continues to expand its multifamily footprint in the region.

The property was built in 2000 and sits on 19 acres with 27 three-story buildings, plus a basketball court and a pool. The sale equates to about $288,000 per apartment. J.P. Morgan Chase had purchased the 530,918-square-foot property for $47.4 million in 2002, meaning the reported sale price represents more than double the original acquisition cost over roughly 24 years. The article does not disclose capital expenditures, financing, or net operating income, so the gross price comparison is the only supported return metric.

The reporting comes from a single Commercial Observer article by Julia Echikson, published September 22, 2026, and based on property records. A spokesperson for J.P. Morgan Chase's asset management division declined to comment, while a representative for TA Realty did not immediately respond to requests for comment. The dossier contains no additional sources, no buyer or seller statements, and no broker or debt information. As a result, the analysis must remain limited to the reported sale price, unit count, square footage, location, and the two firms' recent transaction histories.

The deal fits a pattern of TA Realty activity in South Florida. Last year, the Boston-based firm paid $118 million for a 300-unit property in Pembroke Pines and $193 million for a 476-unit asset in Palm Beach Gardens. On the industrial front this year, TA Realty bought an industrial park near Miami International Airport for $48 million. The Polo Lakes purchase extends that multifamily and industrial expansion, though the article does not state whether the firm plans renovations, repositioning, or a hold strategy for the Wellington asset.

Several limitations apply. The evidence level is single full text, meaning no second source corroborates the property records cited. The article does not provide per-square-foot pricing, cap rates, occupancy, rent levels, or operating expenses. It also does not explain why J.P. Morgan Chase sold after nearly 25 years or whether the sale was part of a fund liquidation, portfolio rebalancing, or a strategic exit. What to watch next includes any TA Realty disclosure on planned capital improvements, subsequent South Florida acquisitions, and whether other long-held institutional multifamily assets in Palm Beach County trade at similar per-unit levels.
