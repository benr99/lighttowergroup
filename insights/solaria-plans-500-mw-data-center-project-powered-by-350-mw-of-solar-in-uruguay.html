Solaria plans 500 MW data center project powered by 350 MW of solar in Uruguay

Solaria plans to invest $5.7 billion in a data center technology park in Uruguay.

Ben Rohr October 15, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Solaria plans to invest $5.7 billion in a data center technology park in Uruguay. The project includes $5.5 billion for data centers with a combined capacity of 500 MW, alongside $200 million for 350 MW of solar PV capacity. The planned solar capacity will not be sufficient to meet the data centers' full electricity demand, meaning additional power sources will be required. That gap is the story.

According to the Uruguayan government, the $5.7 billion project represents the largest foreign investment ever announced in the country. The technology park will be developed in two phases under Uruguay's free trade zone regime, with facilities spread across the departments of Canelones, San José and Florida. Construction is expected to continue through 2029, creating an estimated 3,100 jobs.

The company has not disclosed details of the project's electrical configuration, grid connection requirements, potential energy storage systems, power supply agreements or the development schedule for each phase.

Renewable energy will play a central role in the project's electricity supply. Uruguay already generates a substantial share of its electricity from renewable sources, and the planned 350 MW of solar capacity will provide additional generation to support the data centers. Solaria has operated in Uruguay for nearly a decade, with solar plants in Young, in the department of Río Negro, and Mercedes, in the department of Soriano. The latest agreement marks an expansion of its activities in the country into data center infrastructure supported by solar generation.

I'd bet the missing power sources decide whether this is a solar project with a data center attached, or a data center project with a solar problem.
