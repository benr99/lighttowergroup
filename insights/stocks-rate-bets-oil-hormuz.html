Stocks Stall as Rate-Hike Bets Fade, Oil Break Above $84

Easing Fed expectations offer floating-rate borrowers temporary relief, but the Strait of Hormuz stalemate threatens to reignite inflation fears.

Ben Rohr August 10, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

One chart, one argument

passed $84 a barrelCrude oil price

Stocks paused near record highs on August 9, 2026, as traders recalibrated Federal Reserve expectations. Bets on additional interest-rate hikes eased, according to Bloomberg's wrap of the session, offering a reprieve for rate-sensitive sectors. Simultaneously, West Texas Intermediate crude oil passed $84 a barrel, with negotiations to reopen the Strait of Hormuz—the world's most critical oil chokepoint—remaining elusive.

For commercial real estate borrowers, the pullback in rate-hike bets directly impacts floating-rate debt costs. As markets price a lower probability of further tightening, forward SOFR curves decline, reducing the projected interest carry on construction loans and bridge financing. A $50 million loan with a SOFR-based rate that was facing an additional $125,000 in annual interest from a 25-basis-point hike now sees that cost deferred—or removed entirely. This arithmetic narrows the gap between pro forma debt-service coverage ratios and lender requirements at refinancing, a material relief for sponsors with near-term maturities.

However, that relief is contingent on inflation remaining contained. The climb in crude oil introduces a supply-side risk. Sustained oil above $84 raises input costs and consumer inflation expectations, which could force the Fed to resume its tightening cycle. The unresolved Strait of Hormuz situation—Trump's 48-hour ultimatum from March 2026 has lapsed without a durable agreement—keeps a geopolitical risk premium in energy markets. If oil trends higher, the market's current dovish tilt could reverse swiftly, re-pressuring cap rates and refinancing outcomes.

The crosscurrents leave floating-rate borrowers in a narrow window. The next major U.S. inflation print is the decisive signal. A core CPI above 0.3% month-over-month would likely revive rate-hike bets, pushing forward SOFR higher and eroding the debt-service relief priced in today. For now, the cost of floating-rate debt is steadying—but that stability is borrowed from an inflation outlook that oil is actively challenging.
