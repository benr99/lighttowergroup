The Week’s 10 Biggest Funding Rounds: Crusoe And Fluidstack Lead Multibillion-Dollar AI Infrastructure Haul

AI infrastructure dominated the largest venture rounds this week, with two multibillion-dollar deals in the sector taking the top spots. Data center and cloud provider Crusoe led with a massive $3 billion financing, followed by Fluidstack's $1.5 billion raise. Further down the list, AI inference startup Gimlet Labs landed $300 million, joining a diverse group of big fundings spanning cybersecurity, robotics, food, HR software, payments and healthcare. The concentration of capital at the top of the list underscores how aggressively investors are positioning for continued growth in AI compute demand, even as the broader funding environment remains selective.

Denver-based Crusoe raised a $3 billion Series F co-led by Atreides Management and Valor Equity Partners, with Mubadala Capital also participating. Originally founded to use stranded natural gas to power cryptocurrency mining, Crusoe has transformed into a major AI cloud and data center provider serving customers including OpenAI, Microsoft and Meta. The company has raised nearly $7.2 billion to date, and the latest financing values it at $30 billion — triple its valuation less than a year ago — according to Crunchbase. New York-based Fluidstack raised $1.5 billion in a private equity round led by Jane Street Capital, bringing the AI infrastructure company's total funding to just over $2.6 billion. Fluidstack provides large-scale GPU and data center infrastructure for demanding AI workloads and has emerged as one of a growing group of companies spending heavily to meet soaring compute demand for AI. The financing values the company at $18 billion.

The evidence for these figures comes from a single Crunchbase News weekly roundup published on September 4, 2026, which tracks announced U.S. funding rounds of $100 million or more. The source is a secondary, tier-2 outlet that aggregates company announcements and Crunchbase data. Because the dossier contains only one full-text source, the analysis must remain close to that reporting. The Crunchbase article also notes that Gimlet Labs, a San Francisco-based AI inference startup, raised a $300 million Series B led by Andreessen Horowitz, with participation from Sapphire Ventures, Menlo Ventures, Arm Holdings and M12 – Microsoft's Venture Fund. Gimlet is building an AI inference cloud that distributes workloads across different types of chips, an approach aimed at making the increasingly compute-intensive process of running AI models faster and more efficient. The company has raised $392 million to date and was valued at $3 billion in the latest round, per Crunchbase.

The multibillion-dollar scale of the Crusoe and Fluidstack deals signals that AI infrastructure remains the most capital-intensive segment of the venture market. Crusoe's valuation tripling in under a year, from a base of nearly $7.2 billion in total funding to a $30 billion valuation, reflects investor conviction that data center capacity and AI cloud services will remain scarce and strategically valuable. Fluidstack's $18 billion valuation after raising just over $2.6 billion in total funding suggests a similar premium on GPU and data center infrastructure. The presence of financial investors such as Atreides Management, Valor Equity Partners, Mubadala Capital, and Jane Street Capital — rather than solely traditional venture firms — indicates that large-scale AI infrastructure is increasingly being financed by crossover and private equity-style capital. This shift has implications for how these companies may be governed and how they may approach future liquidity, as the investor base now includes actors with different return horizons than early-stage venture funds.

The dossier leaves several important questions unanswered. It does not specify the round structures, revenue figures, profitability, or customer concentration for Crusoe or Fluidstack beyond the named clients. It also does not provide comparative data on how these valuations relate to revenue multiples or to other AI infrastructure companies outside the top 10 list. The single-source nature of the evidence means that the reported valuations and funding totals have not been independently corroborated within the dossier. What to watch next is whether Crusoe and Fluidstack can convert their capital raises into sustained operational capacity and whether the broader AI infrastructure funding wave continues at this pace, or whether the concentration of capital in a few large players begins to crowd out smaller entrants. The Crunchbase Megadeals Board, which tracks $100 million-plus venture deals to U.S.-based companies, will be a useful reference for monitoring whether this pattern persists in subsequent weeks.
