Newmark Arranges $98M Sale of Medical Campus in Fairfax

Newmark arranged the $97.5 million sale of the Willow Oaks Medical Campus, a 382,850-square-foot, two-building medical office portfolio in Fairfax, Virginia, to

Ben Rohr September 28, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Newmark arranged the $97.5 million sale of the Willow Oaks Medical Campus in Fairfax, Virginia, a transaction that underscores continued institutional demand for well-located medical office assets. The buyer, Inova Health System, carries a Moody's Aa2 rating, while the seller was Stockdale Capital Partners. The deal matters because it involves a health system acquiring a sizable medical office portfolio adjacent to its own headquarters and flagship hospital, a pattern that can signal strategic consolidation of clinical and administrative space rather than purely financial investment.

The portfolio consists of two buildings totaling 382,850 square feet, located at 8260 and 8280 Willow Oaks Corporate Drive. Each property spans approximately 188,000 square feet. The assets have benefited from multi-million-dollar capital investment initiatives into tenant spaces, common areas, and amenities in recent years, according to the source. The properties sit in the Merrifield submarket, adjacent to Inova's headquarters and Inova Fairfax Hospital, with connectivity to the Capital Beltway and the Dunn Loring-Merrifield Metro station. The portfolio is also proximate to Tysons, Washington, D.C., and the Mosaic District, a mixed-use destination offering retail, dining, and entertainment.

The source is a single Connect CRE article read in full, and the reported figures are not independently corroborated in the dossier. The headline cites a $98 million sale, while the body text specifies $97.5 million, a minor discrepancy that appears to reflect rounding. The Newmark team included Co-Heads of U.S. Medical Office Capital Markets Ben Appel, Jay Miele, and Justin Shepherd, Head of Healthcare Debt John Nero, Senior Managing Director Michael Greeley, and Associate Directors Ron Ott, who represented the seller. D.C.-area capital markets Executive Managing Directors Jud Ryan and James Cassidy supported the transaction. No financing terms, capitalization rates, or per-square-foot pricing were disclosed in the source.

The transaction has implications for the medical office sector in Northern Virginia. A health system with an Aa2 rating acquiring a portfolio adjacent to its existing campus suggests that proximity to core hospital operations and transit infrastructure remains a priority for medical office buyers. The Merrifield submarket's access to the Capital Beltway, Metro, and major mixed-use amenities may support tenant retention and long-term occupancy, though the source does not provide occupancy rates, lease terms, or tenant mix. The absence of those details limits any assessment of the portfolio's income profile or the pricing implied by the $97.5 million figure.

What to watch is whether additional sources confirm the transaction terms and whether Inova discloses plans for the buildings. The dossier does not indicate whether the acquisition expands Inova's clinical footprint, consolidates administrative functions, or replaces leased space elsewhere. Without corroborating reports, the deal's significance rests primarily on the identity of the buyer, the asset's location, and the reported capital improvements. Future disclosures on occupancy, rental rates, or capital expenditure plans would clarify whether the sale reflects a premium for strategic adjacency or a more standard medical office investment.
