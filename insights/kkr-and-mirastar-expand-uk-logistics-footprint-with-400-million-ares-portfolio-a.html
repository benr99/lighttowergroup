KKR and Mirastar Expand UK Logistics Footprint with £400 Million Ares Portfolio Acquisition

KKR and Mirastar have acquired a 2.7-million-square-foot portfolio of eight UK logistics assets from Ares Real Estate funds, valued at approximately £400 millio

Ben Rohr September 29, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

KKR and Mirastar, KKR Real Estate's industrial and logistics platform in Europe, have acquired a portfolio of eight UK logistics assets from Ares Real Estate funds. The transaction, valued at approximately £400 million (US$529 million) according to published reports, adds 2.7 million square feet of fully occupied logistics space to KKR's European portfolio. The deal matters because it signals continued institutional conviction in UK logistics at a time when capital markets are scrutinizing real estate exposure more carefully. KKR's willingness to deploy capital into a fully occupied, income-producing portfolio suggests the firm sees durable value in the sector's fundamentals.

The portfolio comprises modern, high-quality assets across established distribution markets in the Midlands, South East and North of England. Specific locations named in the source include Corby, Doncaster, Stoke-on-Trent and Milton Keynes. KKR and Mirastar said the assets benefit from strong connectivity to key population centres and transportation infrastructure. The fully occupied status of the portfolio means the acquisition delivers immediate rental income, a characteristic that aligns with KKR's stated interest in durable income and growth potential. The seller was Ares Real Estate funds, and the buyer was advised by DTRE and DLA Piper.

The evidence for this transaction comes from a single secondary source, Connect CRE, which reported the deal on September 29, 2026. The source cites published reports for the valuation figure and includes a direct quote from Mai-Lan de Marcilly, co-head of European real estate equity at KKR. She said, "We continue to see strong fundamentals in UK logistics, underpinned by resilient occupier demand and constrained supply in key markets. With its combination of durable income and meaningful growth potential, this portfolio represents the type of opportunity KKR's diversified pools of capital are well positioned to pursue." The source does not provide additional financial details such as cap rates, financing terms, or seller motivations.

The acquisition extends a pattern of KKR and Mirastar activity in UK logistics. In late July, the pair announced the acquisition of a portfolio of four prime UK logistics assets from PLP for approximately £170 million, totalling 1.25 million square feet. Taken together, the two transactions represent roughly £570 million of UK logistics acquisitions within a short window. This suggests a deliberate strategy to scale Mirastar's platform in markets where KKR sees constrained supply and resilient occupier demand. The geographic spread across the Midlands, South East and North of England also indicates a focus on established distribution corridors rather than speculative secondary locations.

Several limitations apply to this analysis. The dossier contains only one source, and the valuation figure is attributed to "published reports" rather than confirmed by the parties directly. The source does not disclose the number of tenants, lease durations, rental levels, or the portfolio's historical performance. It also does not state whether the assets were sold individually or as a single portfolio transaction, nor does it clarify the exact closing date. Investors should watch for confirmation from KKR, Mirastar, or Ares, as well as any subsequent disclosures about financing, asset-level performance, or integration into Mirastar's existing platform. The absence of corroborating sources means the reported valuation and portfolio details should be treated as preliminary until confirmed by additional reporting or official statements.
