Brixmor Property Group, Everview Partners Acquire Slate Grocery REIT for $2.34B

Brixmor Property Group, a $9 billion publicly traded REIT, has partnered with Everview Partners to acquire Slate Grocery REIT for $2.34 billion, adding 115 groc

Brixmor Property Group, a $9 billion publicly traded real estate investment trust with 346 shopping centers spanning more than 63 million square feet, announced Monday that it has partnered with Everview Partners to acquire Slate Grocery REIT for $2.34 billion. The transaction matters because it represents a significant consolidation in the grocery-anchored, open-air retail sector at a time when institutional capital has been flowing into the asset class. Brixmor CEO Brian Finnegan told Commercial Observer that his firm was motivated to finalize the deal after seeing a large amount of institutional capital enter the space in recent years and "validate the asset class."

The mechanics of the deal are split into two parts. Brixmor will acquire 23 shopping centers in Florida, Georgia and the two Carolinas for $636 million, owning essentially 100 percent of those assets outright. Separately, Brixmor will form an institutional joint venture with Everview Partners and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority to acquire the remaining 92 assets from Slate Grocery REIT for $1.71 billion. In that larger joint venture, Brixmor will hold a 20 percent common equity interest while still serving as the portfolio's asset manager, property manager and leasing representative. Slate Grocery REIT's portfolio consists of 115 grocery-anchored, open-air retail centers totaling 15.7 million square feet.

The evidence for this analysis comes from a single full-text source: a Commercial Observer article by Brian Pascus published September 28, 2026. The article includes direct quotes from Brixmor CEO Brian Finnegan and Everview founder and CEO Billy Rahm. Finnegan emphasized that the new portfolio is "in markets we know really well with a lot of growth potential, redevelopment opportunities right out the gate, and significant mark-to-market rent opportunities." Rahm described the portfolio as a "high-quality collection of centers in attractive markets" and noted the assets contained upside embedded in below-market rents. Finnegan also stated there is a 70 percent market overlap between the new portfolio and Brixmor's existing assets, with new assets entering across Texas and the Northeast.

The sector implications are notable. Finnegan said roughly half of Brixmor's acquisition activity since becoming a public company in 2013 — approximately $850 million of acquisitions — has been completed in the last two years, focused on Florida, Texas, Southern California and Colorado. The common theme, he said, is markets the firm knows well, upside in rents, and redevelopment opportunities. Brixmor's existing tenant base includes Kroger, Publix, TJ Maxx, Burlington, Ross, Amazon and Whole Foods, suggesting the new portfolio may benefit from existing grocer and retail relationships. The deal has already been approved by the boards of both Brixmor and Slate and is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027.

Several limitations apply to this analysis. The dossier contains only one source read in full, so independent corroboration of the transaction terms, valuation, and strategic rationale is not available. Financing terms for the $2.34 billion acquisition are not disclosed in the source. The specific identity of the 23 assets Brixmor will own outright versus the 92 assets in the joint venture is not itemized. The article does not provide Slate Grocery REIT's prior ownership structure, debt levels, or seller motivations. What to watch includes the first quarter 2027 closing timeline, any regulatory or shareholder approvals beyond board approval, and whether Brixmor's stated redevelopment and mark-to-market rent opportunities materialize across the new portfolio.
