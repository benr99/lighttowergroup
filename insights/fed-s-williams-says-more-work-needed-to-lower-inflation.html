Fed's Williams Says More Work Needed to Lower Inflation

New York Fed President John Williams said another rate hike by year-end would be a "reasonable" expectation, citing high energy prices and AI-driven demand as i

Ben Rohr September 24, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Federal Reserve Bank of New York President John Williams said Thursday that there is still "a lot of work to do" on inflation, signaling that another interest-rate hike by the end of the year would be a "reasonable" expectation depending on how economic indicators evolve. The remarks matter because they come just one week after Fed officials voted unanimously to raise the benchmark rate by a quarter percentage point to a range of 3.75% to 4%, and they reinforce the view that the central bank is not yet ready to declare victory over price pressures. Williams pointed to lingering inflation above target, high energy prices, and demand driven by investment in artificial intelligence as factors keeping policymakers cautious.

Williams delivered the comments during an event in London, where he noted that U.S. inflation held above target at 3.4% in August, with a key measure excluding food and energy also elevated. He said the U.S. economy has shown "remarkable resilience despite significant shocks" and described the labor market as "solid." On the policy path, Williams said market expectations for another rate hike by the end of the year are a "reasonable way of thinking about it, but we'll have to see." He also addressed the neutral interest rate, describing a "tug of war" between fiscal policy and higher economic growth on one side and factors such as demographics on the other.

The evidence comes from a single American Banker article that republishes Bloomberg reporting, and the analysis must stay close to that source. The article notes that Chairman Kevin Warsh said last week's rate decision aimed to remove a "dose of accommodation" from the economy in an effort to tame inflation back to the central bank's 2% goal. Since then, several policymakers have argued higher rates might be needed, and a majority of officials expect one more quarter-point hike this year, according to the central bank's updated economic projections. Williams also sounded optimistic on the prospect of a productivity boom driven by AI, though he warned the gains may not be shared equally if the technology is dominated by a few large firms.

For markets and sectors, Williams's comments reinforce expectations that borrowing costs could rise again before year-end, which has implications for banks, lenders, and rate-sensitive parts of the economy. The article's broader context includes references to what comes next for banks in a post-CLARITY Congress, rising bond yields as a sign of a strong U.S. economy, a 20% jump in borrowing from Federal Home Loan banks in the second quarter, and a new playbook for state bank examiners inspecting AI. Williams said he expects productivity growth to pick up in the coming years due to AI investment, potentially reaching levels seen before the financial crisis, though he added that AI is only a small factor currently.

The main limitation is that this is a single-source, secondary account of Williams's remarks, and the dossier does not include the full transcript of his London appearance or the complete text of the Fed's updated economic projections. The article also does not specify which economic indicators Williams would weigh most heavily in deciding whether to support another hike, nor does it detail the exact composition of the "tug of war" on the neutral rate. What to watch next includes incoming inflation data, labor market reports, and any further comments from Fed officials ahead of the next policy meeting, as well as whether AI-related investment continues to add demand-side pressure that could keep inflation above the 2% target.
