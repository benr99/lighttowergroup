Orbach Affordable Housing Solutions / Meyer Orbach Takes Down Upper West Side's 215 West 88th Street for $51M at $500 a Foot

Backed by a $33.15 million loan from Fannie Mae, the Upper West Side trade clears at $500 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

On the tree-lined Upper West Side block between Broadway and Amsterdam Avenue, 1925 Rosario Candela-era apartment houses with nine-foot beamed ceilings and oversized family layouts rarely hit the open market. Meyer Orbach paid $51 million ($500 a square foot, or $671,053 per apartment) for the 12-story building at 215 West 88th Street, buying in 12.1 percent below the $58 million mark set in 2014. When the deed closed on December 14, 2025, Orbach Affordable Housing Solutions / Meyer Orbach had officially taken down 215 West 88th Street from Brodsky Organization Affiliate for $51 million, with Eastdil Secured brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 102,000-square-foot building last traded in May 2014 for $58 million. Stepping in at $500 a square foot—or $671,053 a door across its 74 apartments— shaves 12.1 percent off that prior valuation, giving Orbach Affordable Housing Solutions / Meyer Orbach a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle. Even better for the buyers, the in-place rent roll hands them a verified 5.80 percent going-in cap rate from day one.

Berkadia Arranges a $33.15 Million CEMA Agency Takeout

How you structure the debt on a Manhattan acquisition matters almost as much as what you pay for the deed. Fannie Mae (via Berkadia) stepped up with a $33.15 million senior loan—representing 65 percent loan-to-value—while the buyers wrote a $17.85 million equity check behind it.

Rather than extinguishing the seller's old note and originating a scratch mortgage, the buyers and Fannie Mae kept the existing mortgage wrapper alive through a Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement, or CEMA. Because New York City levies a roughly 2.8 percent mortgage recording tax on new commercial debt, that single piece of legal plumbing saved the deal upwards of $0.93 million at the closing table—cash that stays in the property's capex and leasing reserve instead of going to the Department of Finance.

From Our Desk

When we look at 215 West 88th Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and lender selection. Orbach Affordable Housing Solutions / Meyer Orbach didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Upper West Side footage at $500 a square foot and paired it with the exact lender bucket built for this business plan. Family-sized free-market apartments on the Upper West Side are experiencing sub-1 percent vacancy in 2026—making a $500-a-foot entry basis with 65 percent Berkadia agency debt a generational hold.
