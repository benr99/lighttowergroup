The $396M CMBS Clock Starts Early at 85 10th Avenue

The special servicer transfer puts Vornado and Related’s Chelsea office loan in negotiation four months before maturity.

Ben Rohr August 13, 2026 2 min read 1 source

Four months before the loan’s December 2026 maturity, the $396 million CMBS loan on 85 10th Avenue moved to special servicing in August. Servicer commentary described an “imminent default,” and Morningstar Credit’s alert called the transfer “an effort to get ahead of the loan’s December 2026 maturity date.” That four-month gap is the part to watch.

Vornado Realty Trust and Related Companies own the 633,000-square-foot Chelsea office building 50/50. Related bought the property in 2007 for $430 million from Somerset Partners and later brought Vornado in as a partner. Loan exposure sits in DBWF 2016-85T and was contributed to CD 2017-CD3 and BACM 2017-BNK3. Vornado’s second-quarter supplement reported 89 percent occupancy. Google, the largest tenant, takes 47 percent of the space after expanding from a 180,000-square-foot lease signed in 2014.

Occupancy alone, however, is not carrying the debt the way the 2016 and 2017 underwriting assumed. Morningstar reported that cash flow ran 28 percent below underwriting in 2025. CLEAR, which signed a 120,000-square-foot headquarters lease in 2021, is still in a rent-abatement period. That divergence between leased space and actual income frames the refinancing problem.

A special servicing transfer is not a default declaration. Instead, it shifts authority from the master servicer to a special servicer that can negotiate a modification, extension, or sale. Getting ahead of a December maturity creates time to test what the borrower can actually pay, what the property can support, and what a sale might return before the maturity date becomes a public default.

Numbers frame the pressure without establishing current value. Related’s 2007 purchase price was $430 million, while the CMBS debt is $396 million. A borrower can own a well-leased building and still face a refinancing problem if income is lower than the pro forma and the debt was sized to a different rent curve.

Still undisclosed are the loan’s current interest rate, debt service coverage, appraised value, and whether Vornado and Related have proposed a paydown, extension, or sale. A 2023 lobby renovation suggests the owners were still investing in the asset, but it does not answer whether they will defend the position with new capital or hand back the keys.

December itself is not the next marker. What follows will show whether the special servicer reports a modification, a discounted payoff, or a foreclosure path in the months before it. For sponsors with 2016- and 2017-vintage office CMBS loans, the lesson is not about this building’s occupancy. A special servicer can enter the conversation before the maturity date—and the room gets smaller once it does.
