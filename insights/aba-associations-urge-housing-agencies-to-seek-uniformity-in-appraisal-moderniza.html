ABA, associations urge housing agencies to seek uniformity in appraisal modernization

The American Bankers Association and 11 financial and real estate sector associations called on federal housing agencies to align reconsideration of value proce

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

The American Bankers Association and 11 financial and real estate sector associations issued a joint letter urging federal housing agencies to align their reconsideration of value processes with collateral valuation standards already established by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. The letter comes as agencies seek to implement appraisal modernization legislation passed by Congress, specifically the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act, which included language to create a review and resolution procedure for consumer-initiated reconsideration of value or subsequent appraisal in connection with a consumer's home loan.

The associations pointed to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac as the model to follow. In the letter, they stated that "a single, uniform reconsideration of valuation process will facilitate compliance because lenders will perform the same process for all loan products and reinforce adherence to the new standards with common and enhanced quality control procedures." The groups also urged the agencies to work collaboratively to develop the new requirements, suggesting that "a comprehensive ROV approach could reasonably include standardized educational resources from the covered agencies."

The push for uniformity carries practical implications for lenders and consumers. A single process across federal housing agencies would reduce operational complexity for lenders handling multiple loan products, while standardized educational resources could improve borrower understanding of the reconsideration process. The letter's emphasis on common quality control procedures suggests the associations see alignment as a way to strengthen compliance without creating divergent agency-specific requirements.

What remains unknown is how the federal housing agencies will respond to the associations' recommendation. The dossier does not indicate whether the agencies have signaled openness to adopting Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac's standards, nor does it specify a timeline for implementation. The exact scope of the educational resources or quality control procedures referenced in the letter is also not detailed in the available source material.
