The Tesla Alum Trying to Turn GM Into an American Battery Juggernaut

GM's VP of batteries and sustainability, Kurt Kelty, is navigating a deteriorating EV market while data center demand boosts non-EV batteries.

Ben Rohr October 08, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Two opposing trends are reshaping the American battery sector. The electric vehicle market is deteriorating: GM sold just 25,000 EVs in the third quarter of this year, Ford sold 6,000, and the long-awaited return of the Chevy Bolt moved only 8,000 units, a small fraction of its already-limited production run. At the same time, the data center boom and the return of electricity growth are boosting batteries of all kinds not designed to power EVs. Kurt Kelty, GM's vice president of battery and sustainability since February 2024, is the executive tasked with navigating those opposing trends.

Kelty began his career at Panasonic in 1993, where he led the company's battery research lab, and later helped build Tesla's first Gigafactory. In a conversation with Heatmap News, he argued against standardizing on a single battery chemistry or form factor. He said GM will need prismatic, pouch, and cylindrical form factors, as well as high-nickel, LMR, and LFP cells. "We design the right battery for the right application," Kelty said, adding that LMR will likely be used in most GM applications. He noted that a previous industry attempt to standardize laptop battery form factors in the late 1990s failed because nobody used the standard.

The implication is that GM is positioning itself for a diversified battery portfolio rather than a single-platform approach. Kelty's background at Panasonic and Tesla suggests an emphasis on manufacturing scale and chemistry flexibility. The data center boom may offer a separate demand channel for batteries that do not depend on EV sales, potentially offsetting weakness in the consumer EV market. However, the dossier does not provide specific GM data center battery products, revenue figures, or production plans.

What remains unknown is whether GM's multi-chemistry strategy will produce profitable EV volumes at scale, given the weak third-quarter sales figures cited. The source does not include Kelty's full remarks on manufacturing structure, U.S. competitiveness, or how GM will allocate capacity between EV and non-EV batteries. The evidence is limited to one interview excerpt, so broader claims about GM's market position or the data center battery opportunity remain unverified.
