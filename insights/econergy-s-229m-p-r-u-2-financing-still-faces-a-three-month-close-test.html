Econergy’s €229m Părău 2 financing still faces a three-month close test

The 342MW solar and 150MW BESS project has a 15-year CfD for only 125MW; drawdown conditions remain unresolved.

Ben Rohr August 17, 2026 2 min read 1 source

Announced 14 August, Econergy has signed a €229 million (US$265 million) financing package for Părău 2, a 342MW solar and 150MW battery energy storage project in Brașov County, Romania. Signing is not yet closing. Financial close is expected within about three months, and lenders will release funds only after the company satisfies a set of project and legal requirements known as conditions precedent.

Who is lending

Six institutions make up the consortium: the EBRD, BSTDB, Exim Banca Românească, NLB d.d., OTP Bank Plc, and Banca Comercială Intesa Sanpaolo România. An InvestEU guarantee supports roughly US$132 million of the main facility, reducing lender exposure. Econergy still carries construction and power-price risk on an asset that will not enter commercial operation until late 2027 or early 2028.

Partial revenue support

The award is the largest capacity allocation in Romania’s CfD mechanism, but it fixes revenue for only 125MW of a 342MW solar plant. Under the award, the strike price is US$57.18/MWh for 15 years from commercial operation. That price is quoted in US dollars while the financing is in euros, a currency mismatch the source does not explain. Nor does the source disclose how the remaining 217MW of solar or the 150MW BESS will earn revenue, leaving a material gap in the project's cash-flow picture against the €229 million debt package.

Across 492MW of combined capacity, the financing equals roughly €465,000 per nameplate megawatt. This figure is a simple blended average of total debt across all capacity, not a true unit cost: PV and battery capital costs are not interchangeable. Shanghai Electric signed the EPC agreement in October 2025 and worked on phase one, which was commissioned in 2024, giving the project a known construction counterparty.

Why the CfD gap matters

Romania’s first CfD round was more than three times oversubscribed, with 1.6GW of PV bids made for capacity; the second round launched in 2025. That competition matters because the Părău 2 strike price is the only publicly disclosed fixed-price support for a project that must now move from financing agreements to financial close and construction completion. Drawdown conditions, not the announcement itself, will show whether the deal is real.
