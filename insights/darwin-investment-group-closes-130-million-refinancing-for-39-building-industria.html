Darwin Investment Group closes $130 million refinancing for 39-building industrial portfolio in Chicago, Southeast Wisconsin markets

Darwin Investment Group announced the completion of a $130 million refinancing of a 39-building industrial portfolio totaling more than 2 million square feet across the greater Chicago area and Southeast Wisconsin. The transaction matters because it pairs a large debt recapitalization with a simultaneous ownership consolidation: principals George Cibula and Matthew Lewandowski acquired the 49% ownership interest held by their partners, bringing their ownership of the portfolio to 100%. The move signals a long-term commitment to the Chicago industrial market and gives the principals full control over future capital investment in the properties.

The financing was provided by a dedicated Middle Market Real Estate team within Wells Fargo Commercial Banking. The Wells Fargo deal team was led by Commercial Banking Leader Paul Mokhatas, Senior Relationship Manager Anu Agarwal, and Portfolio Management Manager Paul Crusen. On the borrower side, the refinancing and ownership consolidation were facilitated by Matthew Lewandowski, Erin Cibula, and Patricia Liston. The portfolio consists of 39 industrial properties extending from Chicago's south suburbs through Southeast Wisconsin and is home to more than 225 tenants, providing significant diversification across tenants, industries, and industrial submarkets.

The announcement was reported by REJournals, a secondary trade publication covering commercial real estate in the Midwest. For the industrial sector, the transaction reinforces the continued availability of middle-market debt capital for multi-tenant, multi-market industrial portfolios in the Chicago and Southeast Wisconsin corridor. The portfolio's tenant diversification—more than 225 tenants across multiple submarkets—is a structural feature that lenders typically view as reducing concentration risk. Matthew Lewandowski's quoted statement frames the deal as reflecting "long-term conviction in the Chicago industrial market" and an intent to maintain a "long-term, hands-on ownership approach." The ownership consolidation also removes a potential governance friction point, allowing Cibula and Lewandowski to direct future capital expenditures without partner approval.
