TF Cornerstone (Thomas and Frederick Elghanayan) Snaps Up 135 East 57th Street for $108M in a 49% Billionaires' Row / Midtown East Basis Reset

Backed by a $72 million loan from Apollo Global Management, the Billionaires' Row / Midtown East trade clears at $254 a square foot and shows exactly where New York credit committees are drawing the line.

For years, the curved limestone-and-granite colonnade at the corner of Lexington Avenue and East 57th Street was a monument to late-1980s corporate ambition—and, more recently, a high-stakes battleground over a troubled ground lease. By stepping in at $108 million—nearly half its 2014 valuation—the Elghanayan brothers at TF Cornerstone secured 425,000 square feet of shallow-floorplate Midtown East tower primed for 350 luxury apartments at just $254 a square foot. When the deed closed on March 14, 2026, TF Cornerstone (Thomas and Frederick Elghanayan) had officially taken down 135 East 57th Street from Cohen Brothers Realty (Charles S. Cohen) for $108 million, with JLL Capital Markets brokering the trade.

Look past the headline figure and the story is all about the basis reset. The 425,000-square-foot building last traded in September 2014 for $212 million. Stepping in at $254 a square foot—or $308,571 a door across its 350 apartments— shaves 49.1 percent off that prior valuation, giving TF Cornerstone (Thomas and Frederick Elghanayan) a margin of safety that would have been unthinkable at the top of the cycle.

Apollo's Conversion Arithmetic at $254 a Foot

How you structure the debt on a Manhattan acquisition matters almost as much as what you pay for the deed. Apollo Global Management stepped up with a $72 million senior loan—representing 66.7 percent loan-to-value—while the buyers wrote a $36 million equity check behind it.

Rather than extinguishing the seller's old note and originating a scratch mortgage, the buyers and Apollo Global Management kept the existing mortgage wrapper alive through a Consolidation, Extension, and Modification Agreement, or CEMA. Because New York City levies a roughly 2.8 percent mortgage recording tax on new commercial debt, that single piece of legal plumbing saved the deal upwards of $2.02 million at the closing table—cash that stays in the property's capex and leasing reserve instead of going to the Department of Finance.

From Our Desk

When we look at 135 East 57th Street on our placement desk at Light Tower Group, the lesson for New York sponsors comes down to the marriage of entry basis and lender selection. TF Cornerstone (Thomas and Frederick Elghanayan) didn't try to fight the tape—they bought Billionaires' Row / Midtown East footage at $254 a square foot and paired it with the exact lender bucket built for this business plan. Office-to-residential conversions only pencil in Manhattan when the entry basis drops below $275 a foot; at $254 a foot with Apollo funding two-thirds of the acquisition via CEMA, TF Cornerstone just showed the street where the conversion floor sits.
