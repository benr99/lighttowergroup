12 Sponsors Back Half of Securitized Self-Storage Debt, While Refinancing Risk Is Even More Concentrated

Securitized self-storage debt is far more concentrated by sponsor than its dispersed loan and property counts suggest. Just 12 of 726 identified sponsor groups back 50.6% of the $24.02 billion securitized self-storage loan balance, according to a Trepp analysis. The concentration is steepest at the top: the largest sponsor group holds 14.0% of the sector's outstanding balance through two loans, while the three largest hold 32.2% across 16 loans. This matters because refinancing exposure is even more concentrated among the same large sponsors, creating event risk that could disproportionately shape sector outcomes through 2028.

The underlying loan and property pool is broadly dispersed. The securitized universe consists of 1,187 whole loans backed by approximately 4,700 properties, with a median loan balance of $6.7 million. Yet the 12 largest sponsor groups account for half of the sector's outstanding balance across just 93 loans. The two publicly traded self-storage real estate investment trusts represented in this securitized universe hold a combined $2.5 billion, or 10.6% of the sector, while the largest private sponsor holds $3.35 billion through just two loans. The largest sponsor alone holds 14.0% of the sector balance through two loans.

Concentration becomes a credit concern when paired with low debt yields. Debt yield equals annual net cash flow divided by outstanding loan balance. Holding net cash flow constant, an 8.0% refinance debt yield requirement would produce proceeds below the current balance for any loan with a debt yield below 8.0%. The two largest sponsor groups have median debt yields of 6.00% and 7.28%, compared with a sector median of 9.02%. Together, those two sponsors hold 23.8% of the sector's balance but 53.1% of the balance with a debt yield below 8.0%. Under an 8.0% refinance debt yield requirement and with no change in net cash flow, those loans would generally require a principal paydown, additional sponsor equity, asset sales, or different loan terms to refinance in full.

The refinancing timeline sharpens the exposure. The same two sponsors account for 63.4% of the $7.55 billion of self-storage debt with a hard maturity through 2028. Hard maturity refers to the fully extended maturity date for loans with extension options and the original maturity date for loans without them. Actual refinancing proceeds will also depend on loan-to-value and debt service coverage constraints, interest rates, amortization, and lender terms at the time of refinancing. The loans backed by these two sponsors are current and are not in special servicing, according to Trepp.

The analysis is a refinancing-risk screen, not a prediction of default or a definitive measure of refinancing proceeds. Large sponsors may have greater flexibility to contribute equity, sell assets, or refinance through other channels, so concentration creates event risk but does not by itself imply distress. Through 2028, sector results will be disproportionately influenced by a small number of sponsor-level refinancing decisions as well as property-level fundamentals. The evidence comes from a single Trepp Blog source read in full, and the analysis does not include loan-level cash flow projections, sponsor financial statements, or lender underwriting criteria beyond the stated debt yield threshold.
