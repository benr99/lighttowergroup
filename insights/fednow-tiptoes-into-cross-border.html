FedNow tiptoes into cross-border

The Federal Reserve says FedNow is 'on the verge' of cross-border transaction support, but the timing and mechanics remain contingent on Fed governing bodies.

Ben Rohr September 29, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

The Federal Reserve signaled last week that its real-time payments system FedNow is "on the verge" of providing cross-border transaction "support," but stopped short of saying how or when such a capability would arrive. In a Sept. 23 blog post on the FedNow website, the central bank described the move as "an important milestone for FedNow participants and their globally active customers," while adding a critical caveat: any enhancement is contingent on "Federal Reserve governing bodies." A Fed spokesperson declined to comment on when the Federal Reserve Board might vote on the cross-border proposal, leaving market participants with a directional signal but no implementation timeline.

The mechanics under discussion trace back to an April proposal that would let FedNow-participating banks tap intermediaries, including non-U.S. banks, to send money internationally. Currently, FedNow can be used only for domestic money movement between U.S. financial institutions. The proposal would allow the domestic service to be connected to other entities that connect internationally, effectively extending faster payments to cross-border transactions. The Fed said "a group of organizations will soon test" FedNow for sending and receiving the domestic leg of a cross-border transaction, without providing dates. It added that other financial institutions will "soon" have access to the FedNow cross-border option, again without details.

The evidence comes from a single Payments Dive article published Sept. 29, 2026, which draws on the FedNow blog post, a Fed spokesperson, and public statements from payments companies. The Fed cited Miami Beach-based Payall Payment Systems, which facilitates bank transfers in 130 countries, as taking part in the integration with FedNow. Payall CEO Gary Palmer said in the post: "As commerce increasingly demands instant execution across borders, we're proud to support the infrastructure that makes this possible for our clients and their customers." The article also notes that feedback on the April proposal has been largely positive from payments players including card network Visa, cross-border firm Wise, and digital payments processor Stripe. FedNow has attracted participation from upwards of 1,800 banks, including JPMorgan Chase and Citibank, since launching in mid-2023.

The cross-border push arrives as stablecoin developments open new international payment possibilities and companies race to improve what has long been a slow, expensive, and complex process for moving money across borders. Fintechs such as Remitly have entered the fray, challenging established players like Western Union. Citibank, a unit of Citigroup, said in a press release Tuesday that it is pressing ahead with improved cross-border service through an upgraded system from Swift, the Belgium-based cooperative that has connected banks internationally since 1973. Last year, Swift embarked on a plan with 40 banks to create new standards and formats to speed up payments and make settlement more predictable. The FedNow post coincided with Swift's annual Sibos conference in Miami, where JPMorgan Chase's Priyanka Rath, head of liquidity and account solutions for North America in the bank's payments arm, said companies are "using AI tools to make sure that we are validating the format of payments, and where they're originating, and if there are any errors that need correction."

The limitations of the current evidence are significant. The dossier contains no material claims about specific settlement models, foreign exchange mechanics, liquidity arrangements, or interoperability standards that a cross-border FedNow service would require. The Fed has not disclosed which governing body approvals are needed, what the testing group will include beyond Payall, or whether the service would compete with or complement existing Swift-based rails. The article does not provide a vote date, a pilot timeline, or a commercial launch window. What is clear is that the Federal Reserve is moving cautiously, using public signaling and a limited test to gauge demand while keeping the ultimate decision with its governing bodies. The next watchpoints are any Federal Reserve Board vote on the April proposal, the composition and results of the announced testing group, and whether other large banks follow Citibank's lead in publicly aligning with alternative cross-border rails.
