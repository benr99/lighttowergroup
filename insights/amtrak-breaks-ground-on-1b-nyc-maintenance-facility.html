Amtrak breaks ground on $1B NYC maintenance facility

Ben Rohr September 04, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Amtrak has begun work on a new $1 billion maintenance facility at Sunnyside Yard in Long Island City, New York, marking the largest major Amtrak maintenance facility to advance into significant construction over the past two years. The project matters because it is positioned as part of a broader capital delivery push in New York, where Amtrak is also advancing the East River Tunnel Rehabilitation Project. The facility is intended to support Amtrak's new Airo trainsets, which the railroad says will replace 50-year-old passenger cars, making the investment a forward-looking piece of fleet modernization rather than a standalone repair site.

The project centers on construction of a new two-track maintenance and inspection facility. Additional planned elements include six new service and cleaning tracks, state-of-good-repair work on 11 existing service platforms, a modular office compound, and reconfiguration of two major interlockings and tracks. Amtrak expects the phased work to run through 2030. A joint venture of Scalamandre Construction and Citnalta Construction will serve as the general contractors, according to an Amtrak spokesperson previously cited by Construction Dive.

The evidence comes from a single Construction Dive brief based on an Amtrak announcement dated Aug. 28. Construction Dive is a secondary trade publication, and the dossier does not include the underlying Amtrak release in full. Amtrak said the work is primarily funded by Congress through Federal Railroad Administration grants, but the dossier does not specify the exact grant amounts, the split between federal and other funding, or the procurement timeline. Laura Mason, executive vice president of capital delivery at Amtrak, said in the release that Amtrak is delivering transformative investments reshaping the future of passenger rail in New York, from the maintenance facility to the East River Tunnel Rehabilitation Project and other projects.

For the commercial real estate and construction sector, the project signals continued public infrastructure spending in the New York City area, with a multiyear construction window extending to 2030. Amtrak also said the work adds onto other major station upgrades in Philadelphia, Washington, Boston and Seattle, suggesting a broader pipeline of rail-related capital projects. The Sunnyside Yard facility is the fifth major Amtrak maintenance facility to reach significant construction in the past two years, and the largest of that group, which indicates sustained demand for heavy civil and rail-adjacent construction work even as the dossier does not provide comparative dollar figures for the other four facilities.

The main limitation is the thin evidence base: the analysis rests on one secondary source read in full, with no independent corroboration of the $1 billion figure, the contractor arrangement, or the 2030 completion expectation. The dossier also does not identify the design or engineering firms, the exact square footage, the number of construction jobs expected, or the project's effect on local rail operations during phased construction. What to watch is whether Amtrak or the Federal Railroad Administration releases more detailed grant documentation, whether the joint venture discloses subcontracting plans, and whether the project remains on its stated 2030 timeline as work moves from groundbreaking into active construction.
