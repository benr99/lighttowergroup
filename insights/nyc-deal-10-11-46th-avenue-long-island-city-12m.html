Directly across 46th Avenue from the Vernon Boulevard waterfront blocks in Long Island City, the OneLIC rezoning turned low-rise pre-war industrial garages into high-density M1-3A/R7A goldmines. Closed on the same afternoon as its $95 million neighbor on 45th Road, Vernon Fee Holdings LLC paid $12 million all-cash on September 9, 2026 to buy 10-11 46th Avenue from FPR Property Holdings, LLC—a 71.4 percent jump over the $7 million price recorded in December 2015.

The per-foot math explains why FPR Property Holdings, LLC chose this moment to harvest gains—and why Vernon Fee Holdings LLC was willing to pay up. At $600 a square foot across 20,000 square feet, the closing price represents a 71.4 percent appreciation over the $7 million trade recorded in December 2015.

All-Cash Land Banking Inside the New M1-3A/R7A Rezoning

Because the existing two-story, 1931-vintage light-industrial building covers just 4,750 square feet on a 5,000-square-foot lot, no traditional bank would size a cash-flow loan to $12 million. Vernon Fee Holdings LLC solved that by wiring the entire $12 million purchase price in unencumbered cash.

The real prize is the M1-3A/R7A zoning designation, which unlocks roughly 20,000 to 25,000 square feet of mixed-use residential and commercial floor area ($600 per buildable square foot) directly adjacent to the Vernon Boulevard corridor.
