Wall Street on track for record profits

Wall Street profits are blowing past expectations this year, the New York State Comptroller's office finds in a new report, with profits on track for an all-tim

Ben Rohr October 06, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Wall Street profits are blowing past expectations this year, according to a new report from the New York State Comptroller's office. The data, drawn from NYSE/Intercontinental Exchange figures, show that broker-dealer operations of NYSE member firms generated $45.9 billion in profits during the first half of 2026. The comptroller projects that if first-half growth continues at the current pace, full-year 2026 profits will reach an all-time record. The figures are not adjusted for inflation.

The report attributes the profit surge in part to the AI boom, which is fueling dealmaking, and to volatile markets. The projection is based on first-half growth continuing at its current pace, meaning the record outcome is conditional rather than guaranteed. The data cover broker-dealer operations of NYSE member firms specifically, not the broader financial sector, and are reported by the New York State Comptroller's office using NYSE/Intercontinental Exchange figures.

The supported implication is that Wall Street's profitability is being driven by elevated dealmaking activity tied to artificial intelligence and by market volatility that can boost trading revenues. However, the evidence level is a single summary, and the dossier does not provide a breakdown of profit sources, comparisons to prior record years, or detail on which firms are driving the gains. The projection also depends on second-half performance matching the first half.

What remains unknown is whether the current pace will hold through the second half of 2026, how much of the profit is attributable to AI-related activity versus other factors, and how inflation-adjusted figures would compare to historical records. The source material does not include commentary from the comptroller's office or individual firms, and no material claims beyond the headline finding are documented.
