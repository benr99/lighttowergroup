When an estate finally brings a century-old Upper East Side elevator building to market after decades of family ownership, every private multifamily buyer in Manhattan sharpens their pencil. On September 11, 2026, 243 East 81 St LLC acquired the six-story, 44-unit elevator building at 243 East 81st Street from the Estate of Fred Mengoni for $13.75 million—translating to $533 per square foot and $312,500 per apartment.

Run the numbers across 25,775 square feet and the buyer's thesis comes into view. At $533 a square foot (or $312,500 per apartment across 44 residential units) on a 4,550-square-foot parcel zoned R8B, 243 East 81 St LLC is locking in irreplaceable Upper East Side brick well below new-construction replacement cost.

JPMorgan Chase Finances a Generational Estate Trade in Yorkville

JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. provided a $3.84 million senior acquisition mortgage on the 25,775-square-foot building—representing a conservative 27.9 percent loan-to-value ratio—while the buyer brought $9.91 million in cash equity to the table.

Fred Mengoni, the late developer and cycling patron whose estate held select Manhattan multifamily properties for decades, left behind a classic brick elevator house with low historical assessments and long-term tenants. Sizing JPMorgan Chase's debt to 27.9 percent LTV ensures positive cash flow on the in-place rent roll while the new owner modernizes common areas and free-market units as they turn.
