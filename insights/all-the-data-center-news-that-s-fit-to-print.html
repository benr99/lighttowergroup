All the Data Center News That’s Fit to Print

Entergy has sued the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette over reporting on a Google data center energy deal, claiming the newspaper published trade secrets accidentally r

Ben Rohr September 12, 2026 1 min read 1 source

Entergy, a major utility, filed a lawsuit last week against the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette, the largest newspaper in Arkansas, over its reporting on a data center energy deal. The case centers on whether the newspaper was legally permitted to publish details about an Entergy agreement with Google concerning payments for a large solar farm intended to power a data center. Entergy claims the information reported was a trade secret that had been accidentally released by the state public services commission.

The dispute sits at the intersection of utility regulation, press freedom, and the rapid expansion of data center energy demand. In a statement to a local ABC station, the utility referenced Google's electricity arrangements, though the available record does not include the full statement. The underlying deal involves a solar farm tied to a Google data center, and the public services commission's accidental release is central to Entergy's legal argument that the newspaper should not have published the information.

The case could have implications for how utilities and regulators handle commercially sensitive information related to data center power agreements. If courts accept Entergy's trade secret claim, it may encourage utilities to seek tighter controls over disclosures involving large technology customers. At the same time, the lawsuit raises questions about the public's right to know how major energy deals are structured, especially as data centers place growing strain on regional grids and renewable energy development.

What remains unknown is how the court will weigh the newspaper's publication rights against the utility's trade secret claim, and whether Google will take any position in the litigation. The available reporting does not include the full text of the lawsuit, the specific terms of the Entergy-Google agreement, or the exact nature of the information the public services commission released. The outcome could set a precedent for how similar disputes are handled in other states where data center energy deals are drawing scrutiny.
