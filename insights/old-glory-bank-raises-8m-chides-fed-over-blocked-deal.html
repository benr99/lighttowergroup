Old Glory Bank raises $8M, chides Fed over blocked deal

The conservative-aligned bank says it is adequately capitalized after a private raise, but its CEO is blaming the Federal Reserve for derailing a SPAC merger an

Ben Rohr October 06, 2026 3 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Old Glory Bank, the Oklahoma-based institution that markets itself as an "anti-woke alternative" for consumers concerned about government overreach and debanking, has raised $8 million from roughly 150 private investors. The capital injection matters because it restores the bank's status as adequately capitalized after a planned merger with a special purpose acquisition company failed to receive Federal Reserve approval. CEO Mike Ring framed the outcome as a confrontation with the central bank, saying the Fed did not present a reason for the deal being called off and that Old Glory "will never bend a knee to the Fed." The episode highlights how regulatory approval can reshape a small bank's path to public markets, particularly when the institution's political and crypto-friendly positioning collides with federal oversight.

The mechanics of the situation are straightforward but consequential. Old Glory had intended to go public by merging with a SPAC that would have listed the bank on the Nasdaq exchange. That structure required the Fed to approve bank holding company status for the entity that would have listed Old Glory Bank. When the Fed failed to approve the application, the bank pivoted to a private capital raise and now says it will pursue a direct listing from its existing bank holding company. Ring said this route means "the Fed will not again have approval rights over a new bank holding company." The latest $8 million raise brings the parent company's total fundraising to more than $67 million, all from what Ring described as "Main Street" investors. Investors in the latest round are exempt from registration requirements, according to Ring.

The evidence comes from a single American Banker report, which includes statements from Ring and board member Sean Spicer, the former Trump White House press secretary. Ring said he believes the Fed denied the merger because the bank is "pro-crypto," arguing that blockchain's decentralized control is "exactly opposite of the concept of a central bank." A Fed spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment, leaving the central bank's rationale unconfirmed. Spicer criticized media outlets for reporting on Old Glory's temporary undercapitalization while remaining "very silent as to how the Fed denied" the SPAC application. The bank's financial position underscores the stakes: deposits surged from $10 million in 2023 to $270 million in mid-2026, and it has signed up 85,000 personal and business accounts, but loan growth has lagged. As of June 30, less than 10% of the bank's $277 million of assets were loans, and it reported a loss of nearly $8 million during the first six months of 2026.

For the banking and fintech sector, the episode illustrates the tension between niche, politically branded institutions and the regulatory framework that governs bank holding company transactions. Old Glory's decision to bypass the Fed through a direct listing could become a template for other banks that anticipate regulatory friction, though the viability of that route depends on market conditions and investor appetite. The bank's rapid deposit growth also shows that a political identity can attract customers, but the weak loan-to-asset ratio and continued losses suggest that converting deposits into profitable lending remains a challenge. The crypto angle adds another layer: if regulators are indeed wary of banks with pro-crypto stances, other institutions may face similar obstacles when seeking approval for structural changes.

The main limitation of this analysis is that it rests on a single source and one side of the dispute. The Fed has not publicly explained why the merger application failed, and the claim that the denial was driven by the bank's pro-crypto stance is Ring's interpretation, not an established fact. The report does not detail the specific regulatory objections, the terms of the failed SPAC deal, or the valuation implied by the $8 million raise. What to watch next is whether Old Glory completes its direct listing, whether the Fed responds to the criticism, and whether the bank can improve its loan growth and profitability after restoring its capital position.
