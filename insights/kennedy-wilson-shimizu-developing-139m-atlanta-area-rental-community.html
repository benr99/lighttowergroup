Kennedy Wilson, Shimizu Developing $139M Atlanta-Area Rental Community

Kennedy Wilson and Shimizu Corporation are partnering on Caldwell, a 382-unit luxury multifamily project in Sandy Springs, Georgia, converting a former UPS offi

Ben Rohr August 31, 2026 2 min read 1 source

Kennedy Wilson is partnering with Shimizu Corporation to develop Caldwell, a 382-unit luxury multifamily community in Sandy Springs, Georgia. The approximately $139 million development is expected to welcome its first residents in 2028. The project matters because it converts an underused office property into residential use, a pattern that speaks to broader shifts in commercial real estate as demand for housing in desirable suburban submarkets remains a focus for institutional developers. Kennedy Wilson's John McCullough framed the move as "maximizing the utility of the land and creating much-needed housing in one of Atlanta's most desirable metros."

The material facts are narrow but specific. Kennedy Wilson acquired the 6.9-acre site that housed a former UPS office in late 2025 and began demolition this summer. The project is located at 35 Glenlake Parkway NE and will redevelop the office property into a five-story luxury residential community. The 382 residences will average 937 square feet, with more than 17,500 square feet of indoor and outdoor amenities. New South Construction will serve as the general contractor, and Dynamik Design will serve as the architect. The source does not disclose the equity split between Kennedy Wilson and Shimizu, the financing structure, or the expected rental rates.

The evidence comes from a single secondary source, Connect CRE, published on August 31, 2026. The report is brief and does not cite additional documents, public filings, or interviews beyond the McCullough quote. Because the dossier contains only one source read in full, the analysis must remain close to the stated facts. The $139 million figure appears twice in the source, once as "$139M" and once as "$139 million," but no breakdown of land, hard, or soft costs is provided. The source does not state whether the former UPS office was occupied or vacant at acquisition, nor does it describe the demolition timeline beyond "this summer."

The market implication is that Sandy Springs, described as "one of Metro Atlanta's most desirable submarkets," is attracting institutional capital for office-to-residential conversion. The project's scale—382 units on 6.9 acres—implies a density of roughly 55 units per acre, consistent with suburban infill multifamily development. The average unit size of 937 square feet suggests a mix weighted toward one- and two-bedroom layouts, though the source does not specify unit mix. The involvement of Shimizu Corporation, a major Japanese construction and development firm, indicates cross-border institutional interest in U.S. rental housing, but the source does not elaborate on Shimizu's role beyond the partnership.

The limitations are clear. The source does not provide comparable rents, vacancy rates, or absorption data for Sandy Springs, so the project's competitive position cannot be assessed. It also does not state whether the development will include affordable units or what zoning or entitlement changes, if any, were required. The 2028 first-resident timeline implies a construction period of roughly two to three years from demolition, but no schedule details are given. What to watch includes whether Kennedy Wilson and Shimizu disclose additional project details, whether the office-to-residential conversion faces construction or financing hurdles, and how the Sandy Springs rental market performs as the project approaches delivery.
