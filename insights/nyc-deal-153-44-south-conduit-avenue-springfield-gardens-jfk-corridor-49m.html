Along the northern perimeter fence of John F. Kennedy International Airport, modern two-story logistics warehouses delivered after 2020 are as scarce as runway slots. Prologis, acting through its 1031 exchange vehicle Prologis-Exchange NY 2000 LLC, paid $49 million all-cash on September 11, 2026 to acquire the 118,906-square-foot Class A warehouse at 153-44 South Conduit Avenue from Adam Gordon's Wildflower Ltd. (WF Industrial VI LLC)—setting a $412-per-square-foot benchmark.

Run the numbers across 118,906 square feet and the buyer's thesis comes into view. At $412 a square foot on a 63,800-square-foot parcel zoned M1-1, Prologis is locking in irreplaceable Springfield Gardens / JFK Corridor brick well below new-construction replacement cost. Based on underwritten net operating income, the trade clears at a 5.50 percent going-in cap rate.

Why Prologis Wrote a $49 Million Unencumbered Check Outside JFK

Look at the buyer entity—Prologis-Exchange NY 2000 LLC—and the capital strategy becomes clear. Rather than encumbering the property with a single-asset mortgage, Prologis deployed $49 million of tax-deferred 1031 exchange proceeds and corporate balance-sheet liquidity to close 100 percent unencumbered.

Wildflower completed the two-story, 118,906-square-foot facility on a 63,800-square-foot M1-1 lot in 2022 specifically to serve freight forwarders and express carriers needing immediate access to JFK's cargo terminals and the Belt Parkway. For Prologis, locking in modern Queens air-cargo footage at $412 a square foot and a 5.50 percent cap rate adds another irreplaceable infill pin to its New York metro network.

When the world's largest industrial REIT uses 1031 exchange equity to take down 2022-vintage JFK logistics product at $412 a foot with zero property-level debt, it proves that infill Queens air-cargo real estate remains one of the highest-conviction holds in institutional real estate.
