Philadelphia REIT Assembles 44,000-Unit Platform With Midwest Addition

Independence Realty Trust and Centerspace announced an $8.1 billion all-stock merger that would create a 44,354-unit multifamily REIT across 17 states, with the

Ben Rohr September 10, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Independence Realty Trust and Centerspace announced an all-stock merger valued at $8.1 billion that will combine their portfolios into a 44,354-unit multifamily REIT. The transaction, expected to close by the end of the fourth quarter, matters because it extends a Philadelphia-based platform deeper into the Midwest and Mountain West while preserving a Sun Belt tilt. The combined company will own 163 communities across 17 states, with 58 percent of net operating income from Sun Belt markets, 27 percent from the Midwest, and 15 percent from the Mountain West.

The mechanics of the deal are straightforward in structure but significant in scale. IRT will acquire Centerspace's nearly 10,500 units in Colorado, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, and Utah. Centerspace had already announced plans in June to sell 12 communities for approximately $240 million to $245 million, including exits from Bismarck, North Dakota, and Rapid City, South Dakota. The companies expect approximately $24 million in annualized synergies from the merger, and IRT plans to roll out its Wi-Fi initiative across Centerspace's properties.

The source context is limited to a single Propmodo article, so the analysis must stay close to its reported facts. IRT ranked 29th on the National Multifamily Housing Council's top 50 owners this year with 34,421 units. The merged entity will retain the Independence Realty Trust name and Philadelphia headquarters, with CEO Scott Schaeffer and CFO James Sebra leading the combined platform. The board will expand to 11 members, with nine from IRT and two from Centerspace. RBC Capital Markets and Rothschild & Co. advised IRT, while BMO Capital Markets advised Centerspace.

The market implication is a continued push by multifamily REITs into non-gateway markets. IRT's stated focus remains on non-gateway markets with historically above-average net operating income growth and lower volatility compared to coastal gateway cities. By adding Centerspace's Midwest and Mountain West footprint, the combined company diversifies beyond its Sun Belt concentration while still keeping the Sun Belt as the largest NOI contributor. The $24 million in expected annualized synergies suggests operational consolidation, including the planned Wi-Fi rollout, is central to the deal's rationale.

What remains unknown from the single source includes the exchange ratio, any premium or discount for Centerspace shareholders, the combined company's leverage profile, and the timing of the 12 planned dispositions relative to closing. The article also does not provide Centerspace's unit count as a standalone figure beyond the nearly 10,500 units being acquired, nor does it detail any regulatory or shareholder approval conditions. Investors should watch for the fourth-quarter closing, the completion of the Bismarck and Rapid City exits, and whether the $24 million synergy estimate is revised as integration proceeds.
