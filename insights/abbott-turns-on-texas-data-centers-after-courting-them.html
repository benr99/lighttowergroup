Abbott Turns on Texas Data Centers After Courting Them

Community opposition is becoming a political cost that governors in Texas and Pennsylvania are no longer willing to ignore.

Ben Rohr August 24, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

One chart, one argument

Published 2026-08-23Axios Cities report

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott courted the AI data center boom. This week, he delivered one of the starkest warnings yet from a Republican to the industry: data center companies “dug their own grave” and deserve the backlash they are facing because they failed to win community support.

The remark matters because it marks a reversal. Abbott joins governors who once welcomed data centers and now treat local opposition as a political fact. Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro celebrated a $20 billion Amazon data center investment last year; this week he imposed new restrictions, according to Axios Cities. The report does not specify the restrictions, affected projects, power capacity, or utilities.

That missing detail may be part of the story. The $20 billion figure now reads as a political liability, not a transactional benchmark. It is the number attached to an investment a governor publicly celebrated, now sitting beside the same governor’s decision to restrict the sector. States are not withdrawing from data center economics, but they are repricing the political cost of hosting them.

Community consent is becoming a real approval input. Developers that treated local support as a public-relations step may now find it priced into permitting timelines. No developers or operators beyond Amazon are named, and the capital impact cannot be calculated from the available reporting. The shift in posture, not a specific project loss, is the material fact.

The political signal is already a form of policy. Two governors are responding to backlash with public criticism or restrictions, and that changes the approval atmosphere even before new statutes arrive. Data center developers can no longer assume that past political welcome will absorb local anger. The burden has shifted to them to earn the community support they skipped.
