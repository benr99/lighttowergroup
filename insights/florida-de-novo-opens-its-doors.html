Florida de novo opens its doors

Portrait Bank, central Florida’s first de novo in nearly a decade, opened after raising $43 million from 256 local investors—well above the roughly $28 million

Ben Rohr September 23, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Portrait Bank, central Florida’s first de novo in nearly a decade, opened its doors Saturday after gaining its final necessary approval from the Florida Office of Financial Regulation three months ago. A group led by central Florida banking veteran Erik Weiner submitted applications to the state regulator and to the Federal Deposit Insurance Corp. just over a year ago and secured conditional approvals in April. Portrait raised $43 million from 256 local investors ahead of opening, significantly higher than the roughly $28 million regulators required in initial capital.

No investor put down more than $1 million in what now-CEO Weiner called a “diversified funding effort.” Weiner said the $43 million raised “demonstrated how much the community was needing this, how excited they were, how much they rallied behind us.” After gaining its charter in June, Weiner’s team spent the summer onboarding clients—small to mid-size businesses, as well as individuals and private wealth clients. Weiner said four banks serve a majority of the central Florida market, and consolidation has cut the number of banks headquartered in the region from 38 in 2000 to roughly half a dozen.

The opening points to a supported implication: local demand for alternatives to consolidated banking may be strong enough to attract capital well beyond regulatory minimums. Weiner said clients “feel like they’re put into a box” amid fewer choices, less personalized service, and less local decision making. Portrait also opens in a growing region; Orlando’s population expanded 1.3% in 2025, above Florida’s 0.8% growth rate and sixth highest among the country’s 30 most populous areas, according to the Orlando Economic Partnership citing census bureau data.

What remains unknown is whether the early capital enthusiasm translates into sustained deposit and loan growth, profitability, or market share gains against the four dominant banks. The dossier does not provide opening-day client counts, asset targets, or performance benchmarks. It also does not detail regulatory conditions beyond the conditional approvals in April and the charter granted in June, leaving the bank’s near-term operating constraints and competitive response unaddressed.
