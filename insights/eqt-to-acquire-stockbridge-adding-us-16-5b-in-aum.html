EQT to Acquire Stockbridge, Adding US$16.5B in AUM

EQT AB has agreed to acquire Stockbridge, a leading U.S. real estate investment manager with approximately US$16.5 billion in fee-generating assets under…

Ben Rohr October 15, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

EQT AB has agreed to acquire Stockbridge, a leading U.S. real estate investment manager with approximately US$16.5 billion in fee-generating assets under management. The base consideration is US$375 million.

The acquisition covers 100% of the Stockbridge management company and the general partner entities which control interests in the Stockbridge funds. EQT AB may pay another US$175 million over the next four years.

Stockbridge was founded in 2003 and is headquartered in San Francisco. Stockbridge has approximately 185 professionals across six U.S. offices and approximately 180 institutional clients.

Stockbridge offers specialist depth in traditional property sectors and subsectors, including industrial outdoor storage, alternative living including manufactured housing, and established open-ended core products. Post-closing, Stockbridge's diversified strategies and alternative sectors will operate as a dedicated business line within EQT Real Estate. Its industrial capabilities will be brought together with EQT Real Estate's existing industrial platform.

Existing leadership, investment teams and decision-making processes will largely remain in place, the two companies said Thursday. Stockbridge CEO Terry Fancher will become vice chair of EQT Real Estate. Stockbridge CFO Kristin Renaudin will become global CFO of EQT Real Estate.

Tuba Malinowski, currently executive managing director at Stockbridge, will become head of U.S. Diversified Real Estate. The acquisition is expected to close in the first quarter of 2027.

I read this as a sign of consolidation in real estate management: large global platforms like EQT are absorbing specialized U.S. managers, because the deal adds $16.5 billion in fee-generating assets to EQT's real estate business.

Will the earn-out be fully paid, or will integration challenges reduce it?
