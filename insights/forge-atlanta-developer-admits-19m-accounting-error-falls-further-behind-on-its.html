Forge Atlanta Developer Admits $19M Accounting Error, Falls Further Behind On Its Loan

Webstar Technology Group revised its 2025 annual earnings report in an Oct. 15 filing to reflect total liabilities of $19.8M, a correction from the $1.1M in…

Ben Rohr October 21, 2026 1 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Webstar Technology Group revised its 2025 annual earnings report in an Oct. 15 filing to reflect total liabilities of $19.8M, a correction from the $1.1M in liabilities it previously reported to the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Webstar, a penny stock company, aspires to develop a $3.8B mixed-use district in Downtown Atlanta. Webstar also disclosed that it has not paid off the nearly $34M mortgage it took out to acquire the 10-acre site for the ambitious project.

I read this as a capital stack that is far more fragile than the project's scale suggests. Webstar said it had mischaracterized a portion of its liabilities and noted that there was a "material weakness in internal control over financial reporting."

The restatement comes after Webstar's previous accounting firm, Pipara & Co., resigned on April 15 rather than signing off on an audit of the 2025 earnings, according to SEC filings. Webstar published its unaudited financials, then hired a new accounting firm that, according to Webstar, discovered the error after reviewing the firm's historical financial statements and related accounting records.

Webstar has been attempting to line up financing for the first phase of the project, a $756M endeavor that would include a 300-room hotel, 600 luxury condo units and 60K SF of retail and entertainment space. If it can deliver, Fulton County's development authority has agreed to give it a $10M tax break and $224M of bond financing. In April, Webstar revealed that it was at risk of going out of business after defaulting on the mortgage it took out to acquire the site.

A nineteen-million-dollar hole in a three-point-eight-billion-dollar dream.
