Mamdani announces $607M renovation of NYCHA’s Nostrand Houses via public trust

The first financial closing through New York City's Public Housing Preservation Trust will fund a $607 million rehabilitation of 1,148 apartments at Nostrand Ho

Ben Rohr October 05, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced the closing of financing for a $607 million comprehensive rehabilitation of Nostrand Houses in Sheepshead Bay, covering 1,148 apartments across 16 buildings and roughly 2,000 residents. The announcement matters because it represents the first-ever financial closing through the city's Public Housing Preservation Trust, created in 2022 to address declining federal support for NYCHA properties. Nostrand Houses residents were also the first to vote to join the Trust, in December 2023, making this project a structural proof of concept for a financing model that could be replicated across other developments.

The rehabilitation will be led by Community Modernization Group and is scheduled to start this fall, with completion expected by spring 2030. The scope includes major upgrades to heating and domestic hot water infrastructure, electrical wiring, plumbing, and elevators. Every kitchen and bathroom will be renovated down to the wall structure, with new appliances, quartz countertops, scratch-resistant flooring, energy-efficient double-pane windows, and intercom systems. Each residence will receive window heat pump systems for resident-controlled heating and cooling, dedicated laundry hookups, and lead and mold remediation. Accessibility work covers 81 apartments for residents with mobility needs and 48 apartments for residents with visual or hearing impairments. NYCHA will continue to manage the property, with more than 40 employees working on-site and a new management office.

The financing mechanics hinge on the Trust's conversion of participating developments to Section 8, which allows greater federal funding while preserving permanently affordable rents, automatic lease renewals, succession rights, and funding for resident associations. The source, 6sqft, cites City Limits in noting that the Trust and the Permanent Affordability Commitment Together program were created to increase funding for traditional public housing as federal support declines. The dossier also records a $100 million annual contribution by the Trust to NYC Scholarship Accounts for children living at Nostrand Houses through high school graduation, via a partnership with NYC Kids RISE. Three other developments—Bronx River Addition, Unity Towers, and Hylan Houses—have since voted to join the Trust, with additional resident elections ongoing.

For the commercial real estate and public housing sectors, the closing signals that the Trust can move from resident approval to actual capital deployment. The project bundles physical rehabilitation with social services through Kings Bay Y, including case management, housing stability support, crisis intervention, and workforce development. Security upgrades include expanded CCTV coverage, upgraded access-control systems, and an on-site security guard. The scale of the renovation—$607 million for 1,148 apartments—implies a per-unit capital investment of roughly $529,000, though the dossier does not provide a formal per-unit breakdown or financing structure beyond the closing announcement.

The evidence base is limited to a single secondary source read in full, so several material details remain unverified. The dossier does not specify the exact financing terms, interest rates, debt structure, or the split between federal Section 8 revenue and city capital. It also does not confirm whether the $100 million annual scholarship contribution is a recurring obligation from the Trust's operating budget or a separate capitalized reserve. The completion timeline of spring 2030 is stated, but interim milestones, relocation plans for residents during construction, and cost-overrun protections are not detailed. What to watch next is whether the other three Trust developments reach financial closing on similar terms, and whether the Nostrand Houses renovation stays on schedule and budget as the first operational test of the Trust model.
