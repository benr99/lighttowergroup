U.S. and UK Regulators Hold Tabletop Exercise on Central Counterparty Resolution

Senior officials from the SEC, FDIC, CFTC, Federal Reserve Board, and Bank of England met on Sept. 3, 2026, to review joint work on CCP resolution, including in

Ben Rohr September 12, 2026 2 min read 1 source
Share LinkedIn X / Twitter

Senior officials from the Securities and Exchange Commission, Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation, Commodity Futures Trading Commission, Federal Reserve Board, and Bank of England convened for a tabletop exercise on Sept. 3, 2026, to discuss issues relating to the hypothetical resolution of central counterparties (CCPs). The meeting, disclosed in a joint readout published on Sept. 11, 2026, matters because CCPs sit at the core of cleared derivatives and securities markets, and cross-border coordination between U.S. and UK authorities is a foundational element of any credible resolution framework. The readout frames the session as one of a regular series of senior-level meetings held since 2017, signaling that this is an established, ongoing channel rather than an emergency response.

The material facts are narrow but specific. The exercise focused on information sharing and communications arrangements intended to support financial stability in the event of a CCP resolution. Participants reviewed recent joint work among the agencies and acknowledged the importance of continuing to facilitate dialogue between UK and U.S. authorities, sharing analyses, and discussing policy formulation in relation to CCP resolution. No resolution scenario, CCP name, or specific policy change was disclosed. The readout lists media contacts for the Bank of England, FDIC, CFTC, and SEC, confirming the institutional breadth of the engagement.

The evidence base is a single primary source: the SEC press release dated Sept. 11, 2026, which describes a meeting held eight days earlier. The source is authoritative and tier-one, but it is a readout rather than a detailed technical document. It contains no quantitative data, no named individual participants beyond agency affiliations, and no description of the tabletop exercise's design or outcomes. The statement that the meeting was part of a series held since 2017 is the only historical context provided, and it is not independently corroborated in the dossier. The readout's language is deliberately procedural, emphasizing continuity and cooperation rather than new commitments.

For market participants and financial stability observers, the implications are directional rather than transactional. The exercise reinforces that U.S. and UK authorities continue to treat CCP resolution as a live policy priority nearly a decade after the 2017 launch of the joint program. The focus on information sharing and communications arrangements suggests attention to operational readiness and coordination mechanics, which are critical in a stress event where speed and clarity of cross-border action matter. However, the readout does not indicate any change to existing resolution regimes, loss-absorbing capacity requirements, or rulemaking timelines. It should be read as a signal of sustained regulatory attention, not a catalyst for immediate market repricing.

The principal limitation is the thinness of the disclosed record. The dossier contains no information on which CCPs were considered, what failure scenarios were tested, whether any gaps were identified, or what follow-up actions were agreed. The absence of the Federal Reserve Board's own media contact in the listed contacts, despite its participation, is an unexplained detail that the source does not address. What to watch next: any subsequent joint statements, changes to resolution planning disclosures by major CCPs, or updates to agency rulemaking agendas that would indicate the tabletop exercise translated into concrete policy work.
