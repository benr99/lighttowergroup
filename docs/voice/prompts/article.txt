HOW TO BUILD IT
- Paragraph roles, in order: "hook" (1-2 sentences, from the picked hook), "stakes", "turn" (the point where the obvious reading gives way to the real one; one short paragraph), "nutgraf" (by the third paragraph, plainly what happened and why it matters), then as many "detail" paragraphs as the facts support, one "meaning" paragraph (the bigger pattern, only as far as the facts allow), and a "kicker" (one or two sentences).
- THE HOOK PARAGRAPH is one plain fact (the picked hook, made plain: no "just", "only" or other colour) followed by one short reaction, a "take" or a "voice" line. The pull comes from the choice of fact and the reaction right after it, never from decorating the fact.
- Most sentences are "fact", each one a single plain claim. Use "take" for Ben's interpretation (at most {{MAX_TAKES}}), "voice" for connective tissue or a light touch of humor (at most {{MAX_VOICE}}), "context" and "ben" only when an entry is supplied above.
- A "take" sits next to the facts it rests on and lists their ids in "because". Give every sentence an "id" like "s1", "s2" in order.
- Paragraphs are 1-3 sentences, under 55 words. Sentences average about 15 words and none tops 30. Start sentences differently.
- The title comes from the picked headline (or a better one built from verified facts). It needs a "title_quote". The excerpt is one complete sentence of 80-180 characters that works as the first line of a LinkedIn post, supported by "excerpt_quote".

FACT RULES (hard, same as always)
- A "fact" states only what its quotes support. Quotes are one or two passages copied character for character from a source "text" (at least 4 words, at most 240 characters). Keep qualifiers (most, about, plans to, expects) and attribution (said, according to).
- Every number, name, date and relationship in a fact appears in its quotes. No totals, differences, ratios, rankings, causes or timing the quote does not state. Plans, requests and allegations stay plans, requests and allegations.
- Never claim to have visited, called, toured or spoken to anyone. Do not mention the brief, scores, prompts or process.
